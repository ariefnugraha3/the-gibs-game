Two traps came with it: a perimeter sample sits EXACTLY on its own shape's boundary, so testing it at radius 0 is a coin flip on the last bit of a `hypot` — half the samples of every ring came back "not walkable" and the contour shattered into forty one-segment runs, fixed by testing at a hair of NEGATIVE radius; and a run must start just after a dropped sample, or it is split across the seam of the sample array itself. **Two more breaks were found from a screenshot after that (2026-08-30, user: "masih ada belokan tidak tersambung dengan benar"), and both are now MEASURED rather than eyeballed.** (5) Where two capsule contours CROSS — one road's cap against a neighbour's offset line — each run is truncated at its own last accepted sample, so the two stop up to a sample short of the crossing and leave a gap of up to twice that; measured, 64 of 218 run ends had a partner 15-60 units away. `stitchRuns` pulls both ends to their shared midpoint, and a second pass lets a leftover land ON its neighbour's endpoint because three contours can converge at a T. **Stage 10 enemy aircraft enter from the SIDE on a quarter-turn arc and never reverse (2026-08-30, user: "jangan ada lagi yang muncul dari atas layar kemudian tiba-tiba berputar balik dan terbang mundur ... datang dari kiri atau kanan saja, kemudian menghadap ke atas ... sebelum akhirnya terbang ke atas keluar layar"; SUPERSEDES the 2026-08-28 cinematic-turnaround rule entirely):** 72% of waves used to be born ABOVE the screen, fly down at the player, then execute a 180-degree reversal to leave through the top — that reversal is what read as an aircraft flying backwards. Stage 5 C2 and each of the three Stage 6 generators pass `ADVANCED_REPAIR_PARTS` to `repairScene`: exactly PHASE SYNC then ROTOR KICKSTART, with completed-board progress surviving abort. **PHASE SYNC (2026-08-19, user request) is the first generator board, and it is the FOURTH design in that slot on the same day — the three it replaced each teach a rule.** (1) **FUSE LOADOUT: a board played four times per run must GENERATE its answer** — its solution was the constant `FUSE_AMPS.slice(2, 2+n)`, so three of the four playthroughs were pure recall. (2) **START INTERLOCK: a board whose optimal play is BRUTE FORCE has no tension** — it generated its answer correctly, but every requirement was printed on the breaker card and a wrong close was free, so clicking every row repeatedly was the *fastest* strategy. (3) **FAULT ISOLATION ("terlalu rumit"): this is a generator repair in the middle of a combat stage, not a logic puzzle — it must be understood by LOOKING at it.** Tree, meter, probes and PAR all had to be read and held in the head. So Phase Sync has **no hidden information and nothing to read**: an oscilloscope draws a bright bus reference plus one trace per phase, with a slider under each; slide each trim until the traces collapse into one steady line, every lamp turns green and the turbine spools up. **Its ONLY difficulty is COUPLING** — moving one slider drags the others a fraction of the same distance (`syncCoupling`), exactly as real machines load each other, so one pass down the panel never finishes it and the player comes back to trim again (measured: every board converges within **two sweeps**). Four rules. (a) **Everything derives from ONE quantity, `syncError(g,i)`** — Hz readout, amplitude readout, lamp colour and the trace's own frequency/amplitude/phase — so the panel cannot disagree with the model, and landing on the mark reads exactly 50.0 Hz at 100%. (b) **Always solvable, PROVEN not hoped**: setting each slider exactly and moving on is Gauss-Seidel on a unit-diagonal matrix, which converges iff `(n−1)·|c| < 1`, and **`syncCoupling(n)` clamps the configured value to 0.9 of that bound** so no retune the user types can make a board unsolvable; targets are also kept away from both ends of the track so coupling can never push an answer out of a slider's range. (c) **No countdown, no way to lose** (2026-07-29 rule) — a bad slide is slid back. (d) **The scrolling waves are purely visual**: they roll on the modal's own `setInterval` (the game loop is paused, the same reason ICE BREACH owns its countdown), the tick touches no gameplay value so a board can never solve itself while watched, and `paintSync` repaints only the scope markup plus kept slider references — never a full `renderBoard`, which would rip the slider out from under the cursor mid-drag (the `paintRotor` lesson). Track-click and drag are both live, the same pairing the wire/chip boards have had since 2026-07-29, and the win layers the existing turbine asset over the success tone rather than adding audio for one board. Kickstart requires clockwise flywheel motion, green-band ignition, then the master breaker; a mistimed ignition only costs RPM. **The flywheel BLEEDS RPM (`rotorDecayPerSec`, 2026-08-19), and that is what makes the green band a WINDOW rather than a counter** — without decay, RPM only ever rose, so "fire ignition inside the green band" promised timing the board did not have and the whole thing was `crank ×N → ignition → breaker`. Two tuning invariants, both smoke-asserted from `CFG`: one CRANK click must move RPM LESS than the band is wide (or the button-only path could step over the band and never land in it), and `bandWidth / rotorDecayPerSec` must stay a human window (currently 2.0 s). The Stage 6 HQ upload remains its scripted story cutscene. Signal Trace time counts toward `stageStats` like the other modal puzzles. Tuning lives in `campaign.signalTrace`, `campaign.repair.advanced`, and Stage 6's `signalCooldownSec`/`encounters.signalAlarm`.

## Architecture (refactored 2026-07-05 into ES modules)

The old single-file `index.html` was split into **ES modules under `src/`** with a **scene system**. There is still **no build step, no npm dependencies, no framework** — plain static files; Three.js r128 + post-processing examples come from CDN `<script>` tags as the global `THREE` (modules do NOT import it). `package.json` is metadata-only (`"type": "module"` for Node tooling).

**→ The complete module catalog, every export, the scene interface contract, the config key table, and the how-to-add-a-stage recipe live in [MODULES.md](docs/MODULES.md). Read it before hunting through files.**

**`src/utils/` is the shared layer every scene and entity leans on** — reach for it before writing a new helper: `pathfind.js` (nav grid + grid-LOS + A* + `navAim`/`turnToward` — all robot chasing), `collision.js` (`slideWalk`/`resolveBlockers`/`blockersGroundHeight`/`resolveCylinders` — the hug-and-slide primitives), `meshBatch.js` (static decor welding), `textures.js` (procedural canvas textures + normal maps), `sfx.js` (every sound + the three music tracks + volume state), `math.js` (`rand`/`clamp`/`smooth`/`segPointDist`).

Load-bearing rules (details in MODULES.md):

- **Scenes:** `menu` (DOM-only) → `survivalIntroScene → survivalScene` or `campaign introScene → stage1Scene → stage2Scene…` (**both modes open with an in-engine cutscene scene**: a fresh campaign start plays `introScene` before Stage 1, and every Survival start plays `survivalIntroScene` before Wave 1 — dying/restarting replays neither), managed by `core/sceneManager.js` (`setScene`/`activeScene`). All mode-specific behavior (collision walls, ground height, bullet blockers, grenade bounds, robot movement AI, radar landmarks, HUD status, drop clamping, win check, restart policy) is behind `activeScene.*` hooks — **shared systems contain zero mode if-else**. Scene files are split by role — `src/scenes/campaign/{stages,cutscenes,utility}/` and `src/scenes/survival/{,cutscenes/}` — so adding a campaign stage = one new file in `src/scenes/campaign/stages/` + wiring per the MODULES.md recipe.
- **The campaign stage worlds coexist in one THREE.Scene** far apart (stages 1–3 at x≈30000 / 60000 / 90000; stage 4 at x≈120000; stage 5 at x≈180000; stage 6 at x≈210000; stage 7 at x≈240000; stage 8 at x≈270000; then Kertajati x≈300000, port x≈330000, forest x≈360000, IKN surface/root x≈390000/400000, and Monas x≈430000 — no shared survival park); `camera.far` 4000 + root visibility + robot distance culling hide the far ones. **ALL CAMPAIGN worlds are built ONCE up front by `stage1.ensureWorld()`**, then `prewarmCampaignWorldRoots()` temporarily reveals each registered root/light/phase viewpoint before `precompileStageLightSets()`. Death reloads the saved active checkpoint; Pause → Restart Game deliberately returns to Stage 1. Stages 1–3 alone use the generic Jakarta cityscape ring; The late-campaign stages own theme-specific procedural worlds. See [docs/campaign.md](docs/campaign.md).
- **All gameplay-tunable numbers live in `config/gameplay.json`**, loaded into `CFG` (`core/config.js`) before the game starts. Read `CFG.x.y` inside functions only (never at module top level). New tuning values go in the JSON, not as code constants. Visual-only values (FOV, animation amplitudes, colors) intentionally stay in code.
- **Cross-module state = live ESM bindings**: the owning module exports `let` + setters (`isPaused`/`score` in `core/state.js`, `stamina`/`dodgeActive`/`dodgeProgress` in `entities/player.js`, `currentWeapon`/`isAiming`/`switchAnim` in `entities/weapons.js`, `scene`/`camera`/`renderer` in `core/renderer.js`). Circular imports are fine and used (weapons↔player, robots↔effects, stage1↔stage2, game↔input) **as long as bindings are only used inside functions**.
- **`updateGame()` block order in `core/game.js` is a contract** (matches the old `update()`): scene updateMode → weapon timers → player movement → weapon state → shooting → grenades → explosions → blood → drops → bullets → robots → win check. Bullets must move before the robot sweep hit-test.
- **The `camera` is a LOGIC PIVOT, not the renderer** (top-down pivot 2026-07-11). Its position = the player's eye-height point and its yaw = the aim direction toward the cursor; every gameplay system (robot targeting, bullet/melee direction, pickups, scene collision, radar, spawn points) reads it and is unchanged from the FPS era. Rendering is done by **`viewCam`** (renderer.js) at a fixed oblique offset `CAM_OFF`, overridable per-scene via `camOffset`. Because the camera looks from the southwest, **screen-up ≠ −z**: the exported ground-plane basis `SCREEN_UP`/`SCREEN_LEFT` is what keeps WASD and the radar screen-relative — never hardcode W=−z/A=−x. Full detail (dead-zone follow, `camBounds`, the avatar rig) → [docs/presentation.md](docs/presentation.md).
- **Frame order in `animate()`:** `updateTopdownAim` → `updateGame` → `followViewCam` + `updatePlayerAvatar` → decor → render. Decor animation runs every frame **even while paused**. **There is exactly ONE global time scale: `globalTimeScale()`** (`core/timeScale.js`) — `animate` computes `dt = dtReal × globalTimeScale()` and passes BOTH (`updateGame(dt, step, T, dtReal)`). It multiplies the two sources that exist (death slow-motion from `deathCine.js`, melee **HIT-STOP** from `addHitStop`) and returns **exactly 1** when neither is active, so normal frames are unchanged. Anything that must ignore slowdown (the death director, the GAME OVER countdown, `Date.now()` fire rates) uses `dtReal`. **A new slowdown source becomes a factor inside `globalTimeScale()` — never a second multiplier in the render loop.**

## Invariants — deliberate choices, do not "clean up"

Each of these was a specific user decision; reverting one is a regression. Detail in the linked doc.

- **The page must paint before the CDN finishes.** The Three.js `<script>` tags are `defer` (they block rendering otherwise) and `#bootScreen` is visible straight from CSS — never make the first-paint splash depend on JS, because waiting for JS is the delay it exists to hide. `boot()` reveals the menu only after config, `initMenu`, `fontsReady()` and one real paint frame. → [docs/presentation.md](docs/presentation.md#main-menu)
- **No mid-game shader recompiles.** Fixed pools (gibs 64, decals 44, blood 72, 3 explosion lights), Lambert/Basic materials, and `core/preload.js` `warmupAll()` exist to prevent hitches. New lazily-revealed meshes or first-use materials MUST be added to the warm-up list. Don't call `applyQuality` mid-game. **PointLight counts are constant WITHIN a stage but differ BETWEEN stages** (revised 2026-07-26): only the active stage's lamps are `visible` (`registerStageLight`/`setActiveStageLights` in world/lighting.js, keyed by each scene's `lightsKey`, switched by `setScene`), and `precompileStageLightSets(scene)` at the end of `stage1.ensureWorld` compiles every configuration while the loading screen is still up, so the switch itself has no hitch. **Never register a global light** (explosion pool, muzzle flash) with `registerStageLight` — those must stay lit in every stage. Counts and the reasoning → [docs/campaign.md](docs/campaign.md#2026-07-26-changes-user-request).
- **Big ground surfaces are Lambert, not Phong.** Stage 4's base/parking/paving/grass/sidewalk/verge/wheel-stop materials were Phong with a near-black `specular` (0x0c0b09-0x141310) — invisible highlights that still cost a specular term per light per fragment, on the surfaces that fill most of the screen. Only the road and alun-alun ring stay Phong (they need `normalMap`, which r128 Lambert lacks) plus the lamp poles (real metal sheen). Same reasoning as the older "interior floors are MATTE Lambert" rule.
- **Robot spawn machines are shared animated hero props, never generic boxes** (`entities/spawnMachine.js`, 2026-08-08).
