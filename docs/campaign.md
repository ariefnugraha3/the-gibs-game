# Campaign mode — reference

**Current campaign contract (2026-08-13): thirteen stages.** Every Stage 1–12 completion follows the mandatory handoff: green `STAGE N COMPLETE` → CONTINUE/Space → Field Shop → Start Next Stage. Stage 8 now continues into the Kertajati assault instead of ending the campaign. Stage 13 is the sole endpoint: its checkpoint survives throughout the complete sunrise epilogue, then `CAMPAIGN COMPLETE` offers `RETURN TO MAIN MENU`, skips the shop, and clears the save. Any older direct-shop, eight-stage, or `TO BE CONTINUED` wording retained in dated historical notes below is superseded by this contract.

**Stages 9–13 implementation (2026-08-13):** the approved narrative, world, gameplay, boss, performance and acceptance blueprint in [CAMPAIGN-STAGES-9-13-PLAN.md](CAMPAIGN-STAGES-9-13-PLAN.md) is implemented campaign canon. Stage 9 captures a heavy transport at rural Kertajati; Stage 10 opens the north route through a port with a visible cargo airstrip; Stage 11 crosses a Kalimantan rainforest sensor grid and waterworks; Stage 12 moves from IKN's civic-axis megacity into the root chamber and defeats the Nusantara Warden; Stage 13 returns to Medan Merdeka and destroys M-0 MAHAPATIH. Boss tuning lives only under `campaign.bosses.warden` and `.mahapatih`.

**Stage 5–13 location-sign rule (2026-08-11; extended 2026-08-13):** all physical place-name, destination, wayfinding, gantry, terminal-name, airport-name, billboard, shop-name and landmark-plaque boards are removed. Gameplay floor markers, interactive terminal screens, railway/traffic signals and door-status jamb lights remain because they communicate mechanics rather than label a place. Kertajati, the logistics coast, Kalimantan, IKN and Jakarta are identified by their authored architecture, terrain and landmark silhouettes.

**Dialogue data contract (2026-08-02):** all spoken campaign lines, the prologue chapters, intro briefing, tank radio beats, and cinematic captions live under `dialogue.campaign` / `dialogue.survival` in `config/gameplay.json`. Scenes and cutscene controllers retrieve them through `src/core/dialogue.js`; the exported legacy names (`S1_RADIO_DIALOGUE`, `STAGE6_DIALOGUE`, `INTRO_DIALOGUE`, and similar) remain compatibility views over that JSON. HUD/objective messages are intentionally separate status text, not dialogue.

## Stages 9–13 — second campaign arc

The five new worlds are procedural low-poly final assets with deterministic static builders: no external models, runtime placeholders, generic city ring, or `Math.random()` in world construction. Their roots are registered through `campaignWorldRegistry.js`; `stage1.ensureWorld()` prebuilds every stage, and `prewarmCampaignWorldRoots()` temporarily reveals each root, chapter light set, boss phase and hazard viewpoint before restoring only the active root. This prevents nonactive worlds from rendering while preserving the no-first-use-compile contract.

- **Stage 9 — Kertajati:** airport operations, tower cutaway, cargo hangar, apron/runway and detailed ground equipment sit inside grass/rice/soil parcels, drainage, dirt service roads, windbreaks and utility lines; there is no external urban perimeter. Capture the flight core, return it to the four-engine heavy transport, defend four spool beats, survive solid jet blast, board and take off.
- **Stage 10 — logistics coast:** a visible freight runway/taxiway and apron share the composition with container yards, automated warehouse, pipe racks/tank farm, defense pier, a small industrial town and sea confined beyond the pier seawall. The authored QCC/RTG rig moves A → transition-safe → B with collider parity. Capture the relay, reconfigure the yard, trace the warehouse, destroy the cannon's three servos in sequence, then extract by armored freight carrier.
- **Stage 11 — Kalimantan forest:** layered dipterocarp, buttress-root, palm, bamboo, fern, banana, vine and understory recipes replace any skyline. Far canopy is opaque/batched; route-side occluders can fade. After the carrier becomes a persistent wreck, cross service roads, streams and the sensor basin; a completed lock freezes its impact point, then the route passes dam/intake/spillway/control gallery to the utility tunnel.
- **Stage 12 — IKN:** the surface is a deterministic city-in-forest megacity with at least three depth bands, eight archetypes and sixty semantic clusters—forest terraces, ceremonial axis, civic silhouette, cultural roofs, garden towers, administration high-rises, transit/skybridges, water gardens, colonnades and root court. The separate monumental root chapter switches without `setScene`, retaining stats/loadout/checkpoint. Upload is monotonic and pauses only during Warden jams; capacitors and couplings gate the independent six-legged boss before the broadcast can finish.
- **Stage 13 — Medan Merdeka:** a campaign-owned, noncollapsing Monas rig anchors deployment avenue, park, ring road, plaza, four hardlines, vault, Jakarta civic façades/skyline and damaged periphery. Offline troops and wrecks are cheap inert/merged shells except the 30–45 encounter guards. Mahapatih progresses siege → personal frame → four hardlines in any order → exposed core; it summons no normal adds by default. The world, Monas and boss wreck remain through the sunrise epilogue.

### Stage 1 & 2 performance pass (2026-08-13)

The user reported Stages 1 and 2 "terasa agak berat" after the second arc landed. Profiling found the cost was **not** in those stages' own content — their 3,545 / 3,361 static meshes already weld into **17 / 13 draw groups and cast no shadows at all**, so the Stage 6 shadow-bucket problem does not exist here. It was in two places:

**1. Every other stage was still being drawn-tested.** All thirteen campaign worlds live in one `THREE.Scene`. Stages 9–13 were already hidden through `campaignWorldRegistry`, but Stages 1–8 were not: attributing every scene object to a world by coordinate showed **~12,000 objects (post-merge estimate) belonging to stages nobody was playing** still traversed and frustum-tested every frame — Stage 7's Bandung city (~5,300), Stage 6's two floors (~790), Stage 5's depot (~3,250), plus Stages 2–4 and 8. Each of Stages 1–8 now builds into its own `THREE.Group` (`campaign-stage1-floor2`, `campaign-stage2-floor2`, `campaign-stage3-factory`, `campaign-stage4-alun-alun`, and the roots Stages 5–8 already had), registered with `registerCampaignWorldRoot`. `setScene` derives the active key from the scene id via `worldKeyFor()`, and the Stage 6 chapters (like Stage 12's) select their own root in `enter()`. In r128 `projectObject` returns immediately on an invisible object, so a hidden root removes its whole subtree from the render list **and** from the shadow pass. Measured on the headless world set: objects inside registered stage roots that stay visible while playing Stage 1 fell from **~47,000 to ~7,300**.

Two rules came with it, and both are load-bearing:

- **Room PointLights stay parented to `scene`, never to a stage root.** The number of *visible* lights is what selects the shader variant, and that contract already belongs to `setActiveStageLights` + `precompileStageLightSets`. Moving lamps inside a root would make `renderer.compile` see zero lights for a hidden stage and compile the wrong program. (`renderer.compile` traverses hidden objects for materials but gathers lights with `traverseVisible`, so geometry inside a hidden root still warms correctly.)
- **A scene that owns no world must not touch the roots.** `worldKeyFor()` returns `null` for the Field Shop, the hack/repair modals and the menu, and `setScene` then leaves visibility exactly as it was — `resumeScene` never re-shows anything, so hiding on the way into a modal would drop the player back into an invisible world.

`buildStageDoors(doorList, cellFn, CELL, H, parent)` gained the optional parent so a stage's doors ride its own root; `buildCampaignCityscape` already accepted `opts.parent`.

**2. Collision swept the whole blocker list, per entity, per frame.** `resolve`/`groundHeight` are called by the player *and* by every robot through AI, clamp and separation. Stage 1 carries 200+ static boxes (168 furniture entries plus barricades, the terminal bank, stairs and lift) and Stage 2 more still. The uniform-grid index built for Stage 6 HQ in August is now the shared **`makeBlockerIndex(blockers, {cell, x0, z0})`** in `utils/collision.js`, adopted by Stages 1 and 2 — and HQ's private copy was deleted in favour of it, so there is exactly one implementation.

| per 40-entity frame | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stage 1 `resolve` | 0.133 ms | 0.005 ms |
| Stage 1 `groundHeight` | 0.068 ms | 0.004 ms |
| Stage 2 `resolve` | 0.167 ms | 0.004 ms |
| Stage 2 `groundHeight` | 0.158 ms | 0.004 ms |

The shared version is **exact, not approximate** — two details make it so, and neither may be "simplified":

- Query results are re-sorted into original list order. `resolveBlockers` pushes sequentially, so a point inside two boxes lands somewhere different if the order changes; cell order alone produced 97 and 140 divergent points out of 19,600.
- The query box carries a margin of the largest half-edge in that world, because the point *moves* while being resolved and can reach a box that was outside the original query. A tighter margin of `min(hx, hz)` (justified by "the push always takes the smallest penetration axis") **was tried and rejected**: 3 of 20,000 probes diverged, because consecutive pushes accumulate. Point queries that do not move anything (`groundHeight`) pass `moving:false` and skip the margin entirely. `rebuild()` must be called whenever the blocker list changes (Stage 6 does it when a spawn machine is deployed or removed).

The margin costs Stage 6 HQ some of its old speed (0.041 → ~0.077 ms per 40 calls, still ~6× better than the unindexed 0.44 ms it started from); exact parity with a full sweep was judged worth it. The Stage 6 smoke assert was upgraded accordingly: instead of "every solid prop ejects a probe", it now requires the index to be **identical to a full sweep** at every prop centre. That change also documented a real piece of HQ geometry: four restroom stalls stand 28 units apart with 12-unit half-widths, so the 4-unit gap is narrower than the player's radius 5 and a full sweep cannot resolve a point at their centre either.

### Acceptance pass — Stages 9–13 (2026-08-13, completing the plan's §14 matrix)

The five stages were finished against [CAMPAIGN-STAGES-9-13-PLAN.md](CAMPAIGN-STAGES-9-13-PLAN.md) §14 with executable smoke sections (`25`, `25a`–`25e` in `tools/smoke.mjs`). Writing them surfaced seven real defects, each now a rule:

- **Stage 12 had no exit.** `root.js` ends the stage through `invokeStage12Completion()`, but nothing ever installed the hook, so a finished broadcast dead-ended. `stage12Scene.enter()` now calls `setStage12CompletionHook(() => beginStageTransition(stage13Scene))` — the hook lives in the **facade**, not in `root.js`, so the root chapter never imports Stage 13 and the single green-finish gateway still owns the 12 → 13 handoff.
- **The Warden could not move.** `updateMovement()` read `w.parts.group.position.rotation.y` — a `Vector3` has no rotation, so the boss threw on its first `phase1` frame. Group (`rotation`) and position are now separate locals; a boss's yaw always lives on the group.
- **A sector blast did not match its own telegraph.** The three warning wedges were drawn from `animT` at telegraph time while the safe-lane test recomputed them from `animT` at detonation, drifting ~20° over the 1.1 s window — the visible gap was not the safe gap. `beginSector` now freezes `w.sectorBase` and `resolveSector` reads it, so what is drawn is what explodes.
- **Stage 11's artillery could never fire.** The scan band swept 1130 units in 3.5 s while its gameplay half-width was 28 units, so a stationary exposed player was inside it for 0.17 s against a 1.2 s lock. The band's visible width is now derived from `scan.safeRadius` (visual = tested), and the config was retuned to `safeRadius` 115 / `cycleSec` 14 so the dwell (1.42 s) exceeds `lockSec`. Smoke asserts the relationship (`dwell ≥ lockSec`) from `CFG`, so a future retune that kills the mechanic fails the suite instead of shipping.
- **A finished stage kept a radio line on screen.** Stage 9's and Stage 10's completion reset the dialogue panel only on the *skip* path, leaving a line pinned over the green finish screen and the Field Shop. Both now reset unconditionally at the finish.
- **Robots and a barrel spawned inside props.** Two Stage 10 warehouse spawn points, one yard point and one Stage 9 barrel candidate sat inside solid furniture. Every stage's spawn/supply table is now swept at radius 0 against its own blockers.
- **Mahapatih's wreck slide was frame-rate dependent.** The death slide integrated `dt·(1−k)`, which collapses to zero displacement on one large `dt`. It is now an integral from a `deathBase` anchor, so any step size lands the wreck in the same place.

Two shared-layer additions came with them: `craneDebug()` exposes each moving container's collider **yaw** (so the rotated footprint can be compared with the visible box), and `stage11WorldDebug().occluders.points` exposes occluder positions/radii. `entities/tank.js`'s mortar-shell materials had `emissive` without `emissiveIntensity` (THREE defaults to 1, above `EMISSIVE_MAX`); they are now capped, because Stage 11 parents that shell to its own world root and the new palette sweep covers whole stage roots, not just prop builders.

`setBossHud({name,hp,maxHp,secondaryLabel,secondaryFraction,state})` is shared by Warden/upload-jam and Mahapatih/hardline phases; all scene exits, death/restart and cheat jumps hide it and clear boss projectiles/hazards. Both bosses are independent entities, never entries in `robots`. Warden score is awarded exactly once before its Field Shop; Mahapatih score exactly once at final completion. Difficulty scales only their durability, damage and recovery gaps—telegraph, lock and projectile speeds remain unchanged, and old boss balance is untouched.

## Intro backdrop — night sky + boundary fog (2026-07-27, user request)

**The problem.** After the cinematography overhaul the user reported an EMPTY background "at the boundary outside the area", and asked for a night sky with a moon and stars plus THICK FOG at the edge — explicitly **without costing frame rate**. Three separate causes, all real:

1. **The city stopped short of its own ground.** `buildCity` scattered towers over `gx ±1980 / gz −1150…1980` and the kampung/trees over a similar box, while the ground plane was 5400×5400 (±2700). Everything outside that box was **bare plain** — and the new shots (`flyEnd`/`flare`/`hover`/`rope`) tilt the camera far enough that the HORIZON sits inside the top band of frame, so that bare ring is exactly what the audience stares at.
2. **The fog was too loose to hide it and the wrong colour.** One fixed setting (near 500 / far 3000) left the void only ~26 % hazed where it began, and the fog colour `0x1b232c` did not match the sky dome's horizon band `0x44525d`, producing a visible **edge-of-the-world seam** where the ground stopped and the dome began.
3. **No shot showed any sky at all.** The cinematic letterbox (`#cineTop` 13 vh) eats 6.5° off the render camera's 25° half-FOV, so a shot reveals sky only when `height / distance < tan(18.5°) ≈ 0.33`. Every shot was above that — adding a moon and stars alone would have been invisible.

**The fix (all three layers cost ~nothing).**

- **Fill the periphery.** `CITY_FILL` 2550 is now the scatter radius for towers, kampung and trees (houses 820 → 1760, trees 300 → 430, towers ~68 → ~112, and the outer ring uses a much sparser 0.06 probability so the skyline still fades to low sprawl the way a real city does). Everything goes into the **InstancedMeshes that already existed** → **+0 draw calls, +0 PointLights**. `CITY_PLANE` 6600 widens the ground/river planes — still ONE plane each, two triangles.
- **Per-shot fog.** `SHOT[k]` gained `[3]=fogNear, [4]=fogFar`, lerped by `shotCam` exactly like the camera (fog near/far are uniforms, so animating them is free). High wide shots keep depth (`establish` 900/2550); roof-level shots go thick (`rope` 210/1750). The invariant that kills the void: **`fogFar` ≤ `CITY_FILL` in every shot** — the haze always ends before the built city does, so bare ground and the plane edge can never be seen. `scene.fog.color` is `FOG_HEX` 0x2c3742, and the sky dome's horizon band is painted in **that same hex**, so terrain dissolves into sky with no seam.
- **Real night sky.** `buildIntroSky` repaints the SAME 1024×512 canvas (one draw call, unchanged): night gradient → fog-coloured horizon band, ~686 **stars** from zenith down to just above the horizon (was 200, confined to the top 35 % = never in frame), a Milky Way band, and stronger city glow along the horizon. `buildMoon` adds the only new geometry in the whole change: a **moon sphere + additive halo**, children of the dome so they behave like bodies at infinity (`MOON_AZ/EL/DIST` = 212°/3.4°/2650, aimed at where the core shots look). **Two draw calls total**, guarded by an assert.
- **Shots lowered so the sky is actually visible.** `flare`/`hover`/`rope` and the `wide` departure framing dropped below the 0.33 ratio. The moon is now on screen for ~3.1 s across `flare`→`hover`→`rope`→`descend`, and the helicopter's departure plays out against a starry sky instead of a top-down view of the roof.
- **Sky dome shrunk 3400 → `SKY_RADIUS` 2900** — a real bug the new assert caught: the `establish` shot puts the camera 620 u above the pivot, which pushed the dome's far side to 4294 u, **past `camera.far` 4000**, i.e. the sky was being clipped. Safe to shrink because ground beyond the dome is always 100 % fog and the fog colour equals the dome's horizon band.

`introSkyDebug()` exports the whole state (stars, moon, fog, dome distance, `domeChildren`) for the smoke suite. Verified: **+2 draw calls, +0 InstancedMeshes, +0 PointLights, +1396 instances** measured A/B against the pre-change module.

**The boarding door is a real hole now.** Until this rework the car's platform-side wall was solid for its whole length and the "door" was a decorative plate hung outside it, so sliding it would only have revealed the wall behind. `buildCar` now builds that wall as **two segments** around `TRAIN_DOOR_X ± TRAIN_DOOR_HALF`, and `stage5/world.js` mounts the shared **50:50 two-leaf rig** (`buildSplitDoor`) on the car group at `TRAIN_DOOR_LEAF_Z`, outboard of the jambs. Because it is a child of `train.cars[0]` it rides with the train for free. It is deliberately **not** a member of `train.doors` — that array stays empty, keeping the "the cabin bulkhead never opens" contract — and its sound goes through `doorMotionSFX` like every other campaign door, so one open/close is exactly one clip. `resetBoardDoor()` snaps it shut without sound for stage reset and for SKIP.

#### 4c. Each shot is locked-off, and the player rides with the car

Two separate defects once made the *station* look like the thing that was moving, both fixed 2026-08-07 on the user's report, and both still hold for shot 5:

- The cine focus and the camera pivot were both offset by `departureShift`, so the train sat still in frame while the station swept past it. The framing is pinned when the shot is cut to (`setCineFocus(locoCenterX(), TRAIN_CENTER_Z, true)`, constant offset, no camera animation at all) and only `train.group.position.x` advances, on an ease-in `k²` curve over `CFG.campaign.stage5.departureShiftUnits`. Camera shake is suppressed for the whole phase.
- The journey scenery pool sits directly on top of the station layout, and `updateRide` was already scrolling and showing it while the platform was still on screen — scrolling rails and landscape drawn straight through the station floor. `updateRide` keeps `journey.group.visible = false` for the entire `departure` phase; the pool only comes up after `finishDeparture` cuts to black. `updateRide` is now only called during shot 5 at all, so the wheels do not spin while the train is still standing at the platform.

Because the shot is locked-off, the train has to stay on visible track the whole way out, so the track bed runs **past both ends of the CSV map** (`buildTrackBed` in `stage5/props.js` — one span descriptor lays ground, asphalt, rails and sleepers for both tracks, so the two sides can never drift apart again):

- a **run-out apron** east of the platform, `departureShiftUnits + 120` units long. Without it the locomotive flies over empty space in the middle of the shot.
- a **100 m west lead** (`WEST_LEAD_METERS` × `CAMP_M` = 700 units, added 2026-08-11 on the user's report that the rails only existed east of the station, which made the west side read as the edge of the world). It is deliberately long enough that its own cut end never enters the camera trapezoid from the platform, and it has **no buffer stop** — the enemy consist enters from the west (`ET_ENTER_X`) and would drive straight through one.

**The rails are fenced off from the city** (2026-08-11, user: *"tidak ada pembatas antara wilayah stasiun dan dunia luar"*). `buildTrackBed` also raises an iron perimeter fence — plinth, two continuous rails, ~100 posts with caps, ~400 pickets — along the **north** edge of the track band (`MAP_Z0 − 4`, just outside the asphalt), running the full west-lead-to-east-apron span so no side of the line is left open. Two things about it are load-bearing:

- **It is pure decor: zero blockers, zero nav cells, zero PointLights.** The track cells are already rejected by `playerStationWalk`/`robotStationWalk`, so a collider there would only add work to every `resolve` call without changing a single route. Smoke asserts `fence.blockersAtZ === 0`.
- **It stands inside the cityscape's clear corridor** (`CITY_TRACK_CLEAR`, so `corridor.z0` ≈ 53 units further north), which is what makes the fence — rather than a building — the thing between the rails and the skyline.

Every bar is welded into the shared static batches, so ~600 raw meshes cost no extra draw call (the "campaign decor is MERGED" rule).

**The player rides with the car during shot 5** (2026-08-08, user report "Major Gibran tertinggal"). Locking the framing is not the same as locking the *player*: the pivot was pinned to the platform while the car body drove east, so the avatar was left standing alone on the rails watching his own train leave. `updateDeparting` advances the pivot by the same `departureShift` — `camera.position.x = STATION_TC_X - CAR_STAND_DX + departureShift`, keeping Gibran's offset inside the car constant (`CAR_STAND_DX` 28 = the door's own offset, well inside the 55.35-unit interior half-length, so he never clips the end bulkheads). This does **not** loosen the locked-off shot: while `cineFocus` is set, `followViewCam` tracks that focus point and ignores the pivot entirely, so the station still reads as stationary. Smoke pins the invariant against the car's live `train.groupX` transform rather than recomputing it, so a future change to how the consist is positioned cannot quietly re-strand the player.

**5-7. The journey gameloop is enemy trains, not car-to-car rooms.** The phase chain `departure → cargo → security → roof → finalDefense` collapsed to `departure → ride`. All journey resistance comes from a train on the parallel track.

#### 4d. The background: a city depot, then the West Java mountains (2026-08-09, user request)

> "ubah background dari stage 5 ini. buat agar depot berada di tengah kota. dan di bagian awal journey berada di kota juga. ketika gerbong ke-3 hancur, masuk ke wilayah pegunungan khas Jawa Barat."

**The depot stands in a city.** It reuses `buildCampaignCityscape` — the same ring of streets, parks, instanced towers and trees that surrounds Stages 1-3 — with three options the older callers never needed:

- **`parent: stationRoot`.** This is the one that is easy to get wrong. The journey arena sits at the *same world coordinates* as the station layout (that is why `stationRoot.visible = false` is what "leaves the station"), so a cityscape added straight to `scene` would still be standing in the middle of the rails for the entire ride.
- **`groundY: CITY_GROUND_Y` (−6).** Stages 1-3 are the second floor of a building, so their city sits 70 units below the deck. This depot is on the ground, so the street is only just below its slab.
- **`corridor: {z0, z1}`.** The stock ring only clears a box around the building footprint, which leaves the lots *east and west* of the map free — exactly where the two tracks and the run-out apron run. The corridor spans `CITY_TRACK_CLEAR` (90 units) beyond each track axis and is enforced at every x, for buildings, parks and trees alike. Because the tree layer is a fixed-count `InstancedMesh`, a tree that still lands in the corridor after six draws is *pushed to the corridor edge* rather than skipped — skipping would leave an identity matrix, i.e. a cone at world origin. The builder counts any object that ends up inside the corridor and returns it as `corridorHits`; smoke asserts it is 0.

Stage 5 consequently calls **`enterCityEnv()`** instead of `exitCityEnv()`: the global burning-sky dome is hidden, the background becomes the cool night haze and the fog widens, which is what makes the ring visible past the depot walls. Stage 6 calls `enterCityEnv()` too since 2026-08-10 (its own city section below), so the apocalyptic dome now returns only for Stage 4.

**The journey is two acts.** `updateJourneyScenery` used to run a five-phase table (`jakarta → industrial → countryside → tunnel → mountains → bandung`) keyed to fractions of `routeK`. It now runs `city → mountains → tunnel → mountains → bandung`, and the city→mountains cut is driven by a **car count**, not a fraction: `CFG.campaign.stage5.scenery.mountainAfterCars` (3) is converted by `sceneryMountainK()` into `3 / ET_CARGO_CARS` and passed in as `mountainK`. Since `routeK()` is exactly `etCarsKilled / ET_CARGO_CARS`, the landscape changes on the frame the third car is recorded destroyed, and it stays correct if the consist length is ever retuned. The tunnel became a beat *inside* the mountain act — that is what the Jakarta–Bandung line actually is — and the closing `bandung` act returns to city silhouettes because the destination is a city.

Fixed preallocation is a project invariant, so the swap cannot build anything. **Every mid module carries both landscapes as child groups** (`cityG`/`hillG`) and every far module carries `skyG` beside `ridgeG`. Changing act only writes `visible`, and `trainJourneyDebug()` reports `midCity`/`midHill`/`farSky`/`farRidge` so smoke can pin which landscape is on screen at each kill. The threshold sets the *target* act only — how a module actually adopts it is section 4g.

#### 4e. Filling the journey — the pool is welded, so density is cheap (2026-08-09, user request)

> "background ketika di perjalanan kereta masih terlalu kosong. PENUHI DENGAN PROPERTY YANG SESUAI!!!"

The first pass at the two acts kept the pool's original shape: one mid module every 84 units holding **two or three meshes on one side of the line**, alternating sides. The track itself was two smooth rails on a bed — no sleepers, no lineside furniture at all.

But the real reason it looked empty is worse than sparseness: **most of the pool was built where the camera cannot see it.** `groundViewExtents(eyeHeight, 0)` reports the visible ground trapezoid as `z ∈ [−267, +118]`, `x ∈ [−118, +267]` relative to the player, with a *diagonal* far edge (`x − z ≈ 226`) because the camera looks along the SW→NE axis. The consequences:

- The whole `far` layer sat **370 units behind the rails**, where the height budget is *negative*. It never rendered a single pixel — the game had no horizon layer at all.
- Half of `mid` alternated to **+z at 95…197**, past the bottom edge (`z ≈ x + 96`). Also invisible.
- What was left was a handful of objects on the −z side.

The camera's top edge also **clips height by ~0.35 per unit of depth** — about 54 units tall at z = −70, 40 at −110, 23 at −160, and 0 at −226 (more when a module is behind the train, less when ahead). That is not a reason to build short: a 200-unit tower at z = −120 is clipped by the frame edge and therefore *fills* the upper screen. It is a reason never to build past the trapezoid, where the base itself is off-frame.

The layout that follows:

- **−z (up-screen)** is the main stage: all backdrop content in **−76…−200**, behind the enemy consist at −42, with the horizon band at ≈ **−196**.
- **+z (down-screen)** gets a thin **foreground band at 84…96** — beyond the camera's own ground point, so it physically cannot occlude the player's car, but still fills the bottom of the frame. The space between is left to the enemy track and the merging highway.

What each pool now carries:

| Pool | Parallax | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `near` | 1.0 | Ballast bed, shoulders and drains, four rails, **sleepers on both tracks** (the rhythm is what sells the speed), a concrete cable trough, lineside fences on both boundaries, signal/telecom poles, km posts, relay cabinets, block signals with a lit head, ballast piles — plus the +z foreground band (trees, palms, a low store shed, a pitched-roof house, bush clumps). ~45 meshes per module. |
| `mid` | 0.62 | A full block of scenery per module, both acts. `cityG`: a row of three shophouses, two taller office blocks, a tower, a lit billboard and a boundary wall. `hillG`: four rising **terraced paddy shelves**, four trees, a bamboo clump, a pitched-roof saung and a rock outcrop over a retaining wall. |
| `far` | 0.40 | 12 modules (was 10), **all on the −z side and moved from −370 to ≈−196**, i.e. inside the trapezoid at last. `skyG` is five towers with lit crowns; `ridgeG` is three overlapping green volcanic cones; both are clipped by the frame's top edge, which is what fills it. Parallax was raised from 0.22 because the layer now sits just behind `mid` and would otherwise read as sliding the wrong way. Module spacing equals the wrap span exactly, so the horizon has no gap. |

Density like that would normally cost draw calls, so **every module (and every act variant) is welded with `mergeObjectInPlace` at build time**, exactly like the Stage 4 cars and the combat gunship. The result is one mesh *per material per group*: the pool holds ~1690 raw meshes but draws **~305**, and only the active act is drawn at all. Two rules fall out of that and must not be "cleaned up":

- Anything physically close to the rails belongs in `near`, not `mid` — a foreground prop moving at 0.62 parallax visibly slides slower than the ground under it.
- Long boundary props (retaining walls, the cable trough, fence wires, the ballast bed) are **exactly `NEAR_STEP` wide**, so neighbouring modules abut into one continuous run. Anything wider overlaps its neighbour, and two coplanar tops at the same height z-fight. The paddy shelves are narrower than the module for the same reason.

Module content is varied by a **deterministic hash of the module index**, never `Math.random()`: this pool is built during loading alongside every other campaign world, and consuming global RNG there shifts the random placement of unrelated stages.

`MESH_CAP.TrainSceneryPool` is deliberately loose (2000) because the headless harness cannot weld (`canMerge()` is false there, so it counts raw meshes). The real guards are two smoke tests: **`S5 LANSKAP: biaya draw call`**, which reads `journeySceneryDebug()` and fails if the welded count passes 400 or stops being a third of the raw count; and **`S5 LANSKAP: tidak ada prop yang dibangun di luar tapak pandang kamera`**, which walks every pool mesh and fails if any of them lands outside `groundViewExtents` — the exact regression that hid the old horizon layer.

#### 4f. The journey has a ground surface — grass and soil, never the background (2026-08-09, user request)

> "kemudian warna tanahnya jangan biru muda seperti itu. pakai kombinasi warna hijau rumput dan coklat tanah saja."

The pale blue was **not a material**. Outside the ballast bed the journey had *no ground at all*, so everything below the scenery was `scene.background` — the cool city haze `0x2b3742` that `enterCityEnv()` installs (section 4d). Adding density in 4e made that more obvious, not less: props stood on nothing.

The near pool (parallax 1.0 — the ground must move at exactly the ground's speed) now carries the surface:

- **Two bands, not one slab.** The rails sit in a shallow **cutting**: the ballast formation is around y −5 while every lineside prop has stood at y 0 since the pool was written. So the terrain surface is **y = 0**, and the track corridor between `CUT_FAR` and `CUT_NEAR` is deliberately left open — continuing the bands across it would bury the drains and the ballast shoulders.
- **Brown body, green cap.** Each band is a `PAL.wood` soil block from −6.6 up to −1.2 with a thin `grass` cap (a darker shade derived from `PAL.leaf`, so tree canopies still read against it) from −1.2 to 0. That is what makes the cut face toward the rails read as **brown earth**, not a wall of grass. The two boxes abut rather than overlap: coplanar side faces would z-fight.
- **Soil plots on top.** Two or three `PAL.wood` patches per module, placed by the same deterministic index hash, break the field up so it does not read as one flat plate at speed. They sit 0.3 above the grass cap, again to avoid coplanar tops.
- The bands span `z −232 … CUT_FAR` and `CUT_NEAR … +118` — the full depth of the camera trapezoid, so the horizon is ground, not haze — and are exactly `NEAR_STEP` wide so neighbouring modules abut.

Everything that used to sit *inside* the corridor moved out to the `LS_FAR`/`LS_NEAR` lineside rows (fences, poles, km posts, relay cabinets, block signals). With no ground drawn, a prop floating 5 units over the ballast was invisible; with ground drawn it is not. The cable trough is the one deliberate exception — it belongs on the shoulder.

The camera-side row is deliberately tight against the cut, because that side is **shared with the merging highway**: from the fifth car the road converges to `highway.nearZ` (62) with its lamp posts at ≈32, so anything set farther out ends up standing in the carriageway. Big props (the ballast piles) go on the backdrop side instead of being squeezed between the railway fence and the guardrail, and a smoke test (`S5 TANAH: tak ada perabot tepi rel yang berdiri di badan jalan raya`) checks it from both pools' constants — they are separate pools, so nothing else would catch it.

Grass is a new material, so the welded cost went 305 → **323** draw calls for 1790 raw meshes. Two smoke tests pin the result: **`S5 TANAH: perjalanan punya permukaan tanah sungguhan`** derives the required coverage from `groundViewExtents` and fails if either band stops short or the corridor gap grows past the ballast formation, and **`S5 TANAH: hanya hijau rumput + coklat tanah`** sweeps every ground slab's colour channels and rejects any hue that is not green or brown.

#### 4g. Act changes travel down the line — nothing ever changes in view (2026-08-09, user request)

> "bikin transisi yang mulus dong dari background perkotaan ke pegunungan ke perkotaan lagi. sekarang terlihat aneh karena tiba-tiba berubah."

The first implementation toggled `visible` on all 18 mid and 12 far modules **in the same frame**, so the entire horizon changed at once, right in front of the player. The phase table was correct; the delivery was not.

The rule now is: **a module may only change act while it is off screen.** Two things make that happen.

1. **Wrap adoption.** `wrap()` takes an `onWrap` callback and `adoptAct` runs on it. A module that scrolls off the back is reborn at the head of the pool, hundreds of units ahead of the camera, and takes the current target act there. So new landscape only ever *arrives from the distance*.
2. **A single relayout at the threshold.** Wrap alone is not enough: one rotation of the `far` pool takes ~47 s at `trainSpeed` 92, so the change would hang half-finished. When the target act flips, `relayoutAhead()` re-dresses every module that is already parked **beyond `SCENERY_OFFSCREEN_AHEAD` (420)** — outside the camera's `maxX` (267) plus a module half-width, so the rewrite is invisible. Modules on screen or already behind are left alone; they change when they wrap.

The nearest few of those relaid modules take a **dither pattern** (`[0,1,0,1,1,0,1,1]`, 1 = new act) instead of the new act outright, so the boundary reads as the old landscape *thinning out* rather than a straight line where the city stops and the mountains start. The far pool is deliberately **not** dithered: a horizon silhouette has to move as one line, not a saw edge.

Measured on a standalone pool at `trainSpeed` 92: the old act is gone from the screen in **~13 s**, the two acts coexist somewhere in the frame for **~25 s**, and the Bandung-bound return to city behaves identically. Four smoke tests hold it: no module may change act while inside the view (`0 kejadian`), the relayout threshold must actually be outside `groundViewExtents`, both acts must coexist for at least 8 s, and the old act must be clear of the screen between 3 s and 30 s — the upper bound is what catches a regression back to "waits for a full pool rotation", the lower bound catches a regression back to a single-frame flip.

#### 4h. The finish is its own four-shot cutscene (2026-08-09, user request)

> "bikin cutscene terpisah dong buat finishnya. jadi nanti ada transisi lagi setelah semua robot dikalahkan -> tunggu 3 detik -> masuk cutscene. […] setiap transisi menggunakan cut. tidak pakai pergerakan kamera ataupun fadein fade out."

`arrival.js` used to be a single moving camera that eased across the braking train while two radio lines played, then faded to black. It is now the mirror of the departure cutscene — four locked-off shots, hard cuts, no fade anywhere inside:

| Shot | Camera (offset from focus) | What moves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | extreme close-up **in front of the locomotive**, `{40, −CAM_LOOK_DROP, 17}` off the **nose** | The train brakes to a dead stop over `stopSec`, then `stopTrainLoop()` kills the train sound. Brake sparks run while it is still rolling, and the terminal slides in with the ground. |
| 2 | `{24, 30, 42}` on the boarding door — the departure shot's framing | The two door leaves slide open; one `door-open.mp3` through the shared rig. |
| 3 | `{16, 40, 54}` on the midpoint of the walk | Gibran walks out of the car onto the arrival platform (the pivot moves, so the avatar plays its real step cycle). |
| 4 | extreme close-up **in front of Gibran**, `{7, 19, 22}` | `arrivedCommand` / `arrivedGibran` type out with the radio pose; then the shot holds `endHoldSec` (3 s) before the stage closes. |

Details that are load-bearing:

- **Shot 1 focuses the loco NOSE, not its centre.** Half a car is 57.75 units, so a camera 40 units in front of the *centre* would sit inside the locomotive body. `engineNose()` = `locoCenterX() + TRAIN_CAR_LENGTH / 2`, and smoke pins the focus to it.
- **Shot 1 is level with the train, not looking down at it** (user: *"buat agar kameranya menyorot sejajar dengan kereta. bukan dari atas seperti sekarang"*). `followViewCam` always aims at `camFocus.y − CAM_LOOK_DROP`, so putting the camera at *that same height* — `y: -CAM_LOOK_DROP` — makes the sight line exactly horizontal, zero degrees, rather than merely low. `CAM_LOOK_DROP` is exported from `renderer.js` for this: copying the number would let a change there silently tilt the shot. The frame centre is fixed at that height by the same rule, so the loco's face fills the frame from just above coupler height and its roof runs off the top edge — which is what an extreme close-up of a locomotive should do.
- **The station arrives; it does not ride along.** During the journey illusion the *train* is what stands still in world space while the scenery scrolls, so a terminal pinned at `baseX` also stands still **relative to the train** — on screen it reads as glued to the locomotive while the world sweeps past (user: *"pastikan stasiun tujuan tidak ikut bergeser mengikuti kereta"*). `journey.arrivalDx` fixes it: `dockArrivalTerminal()` places the terminal exactly one braking distance ahead — `v0 × stopSec / 2`, the integral of the smoothstep brake curve — and `updateJourneyScenery` walks it back at the `near` pool's parallax 1.0, so it decelerates with the train and lands on its home coordinate the instant the train stops. This is *not* a repeal of the static-terminal rule: it never joins the wrap, and smoke checks both that the approach distance matches the analytic remaining travel and that the final x is bit-for-bit `baseX`.
- **The cutscene owns the train speed.** `updateRide` no longer decays speed in the `arrival` phase (its exponential approach never actually reached zero); `setTrainSpeed()` drives it down a smoothstep across `stopSec` and snaps to 0. `addCamShake` is also skipped in that phase, because all four shots must be locked off — smoke resets the shake accumulator and fails if anything nudges it.
- **The arrival platform had to be built.** The `BANDUNG_MAP` platform (rows 10-15) is on the **−z** side — the backdrop, *behind* the train from the oblique camera — while the car's one door faces **+z**. A cutscene of Gibran getting off needs a platform on the door's side, so `buildBandungTerminal` now also lays a camera-side apron (`B_APRON_Z0`…`B_APRON_Z1` = 18…72): deck, hazard safety line, low benches. It is deliberately **flat — no canopy**: anything tall there stands exactly in the sight line of the two shots that look back from +z.
- **The camera-side lineside row had to move out of the way** (user: *"jauhkan pagar pembatas yang ada di kanan kereta karena sekarang major gibran berjalan menembusnya"*). The boundary fence sat at `LS_NEAR` = 30 — exactly where Gibran now stands after alighting, so he walked straight through it. That narrow strip turned out to be claimed by three other things: the arrival apron (18…72), the merging highway (asphalt 44…80, lamp posts ≈32), and the camera's own sight line to the player's car (anything below z ≈ 71 can occlude it). So **every railway prop moved to the backdrop row** — km posts and relay cabinets join the fence, poles, block signals and ballast piles on −z — and the only thing left on the camera side is the foreground band's own boundary fence at `FG0 − 2` (82), well clear of all three. Smoke fails if any near-pool prop stands over the apron band or on the road.
- **No fade at the end either.** `finishArrival()` clears the letterbox and calls `beginStageTransition(stage6Scene)` directly, so `STAGE 5 COMPLETE` cuts in. `arrivalMinSec` is deleted from the config, replaced by the `arrival` block (`stopSec`, `frontSec`, `doorOpenSec`, `alightSec`, `radioMinSec`, `endHoldSec`) exactly as the departure rework replaced `departureMinSec`. `S5_ENGINE`, the old arrival framing anchor, lost its last consumer and is deleted.

The cutscene lives in **`cutscenes/stage5/finish.js`** (user: *"lebih baik cutscene itu dijadikan file terpisah"*). It was written into `arrival.js`, which contained nothing else, so the module is now named for what it holds — the sibling of `departure.js`. The gameplay phase is still `arrival`, because that names the *world state* (the train is arriving) that `updateRide` and `RIDE_PHASES` branch on; only the file, the scene id (`campaign-5-finish`) and the debug field (`stage5Debug().finish`) changed.

Smoke reuses the departure's `s5RunShot` harness (its shot accessor is now a parameter) and adds twelve asserts: the four cuts each change both angle and focus in one frame with the curtain never opaque, shot 1's camera height equals `-CAM_LOOK_DROP` so the sight line is provably horizontal, the train really reaches speed 0 with the loop off and the terminal visible, the terminal's approach distance matches the integral of the brake curve and it never once moves backwards before landing exactly on `baseX`, nothing stands on the apron band, the door opens once, Gibran ends up between the apron's two edges having only ever walked outward, both radio lines type in order with two distinct gestures, and the gap between the dialogue going idle and the stage completing is `endHoldSec` (measured 3.05 s).

#### 4i. Five fixes from the playtest pass (2026-08-09, user reports)

Each of these turned out to be a rule worth writing down, not a nudge.

**Nothing static may span the boarding opening.** *"di atas pintu gerbong ada besi yang melintang dan kepala major gibran menembus itu"*, then *"masih ada besi melintang yang tidak ikut terbuka"*. The car's side wall is chest-high on purpose (the oblique camera has to see the player inside a 4 m body), so **anything crossing the door opening sits at head or shoulder height** for whoever stands in it. There were two offenders:

- The **frame lintel**. `buildSplitDoor` gained an opt-in `opts.headRail`; each leaf carries half the bar as a child, so it splits and travels with the doors. The static one in `buildCar` is deleted.
- The **platform-side top sill**, found on the second pass. It ran the *full length* of the car at y 9.0-10.2 — straight across the opening at shoulder height. It is now emitted in two segments around the opening, exactly like the wall itself, and the leaves' head rail is sized to the same 1.2-unit band so the sill still reads continuous when the door is shut.

The second one is the interesting failure: the first smoke guard tested each mesh's **centre** against the door's x-range, and the sill's centre is at x 0 — nowhere near the door. It passed while the bar was plainly there. The guard now measures **x-span overlap** with the opening (with a 2-unit tolerance so the jamb posts are allowed), restricted to the platform side and to anything tall enough to hit a body. Reverting the sill split makes it fail with `112x1@y9.6`, naming the offending bar.

Stage 1-3 and Stage 6 doors are untouched — their openings are wall-height, so `headRail` stays off.

**A pivot carried by a vehicle is not a walk cycle.** *"ketika kereta berjalan, major gibran malah terlihat sedang berlari."* `updatePlayerAvatar` derives gait from pivot displacement per frame, and the departing shot deliberately rides the pivot along with the car (that fix is from 2026-08-08 — pinning it left Gibran standing on the rails). `setAvatarCarried(true)` tells the rig that this displacement is not self-locomotion: position still updates, the step cycle does not. `finishDeparture` clears it.

**The foreground band is extinguished when the highway arms.** *"ketika transisi jalan raya masuk, masih banyak rumah pohon dan objek lainnya yang ada di tengah jalan."* The road merges from `farZ` 200 to `nearZ` 62, so during the approach it sweeps **through** the camera-side foreground band at 84…96 — houses and trees end up standing in the asphalt. That band is now its own welded child group per near module (`g.userData.fgG`, beside `mainG`), and `setJourneyForeground()` — called from `startHighway`/`stopHighway` — clears it with exactly the machinery the landscape acts use: a one-shot for every module already parked beyond `SCENERY_OFFSCREEN_AHEAD`, then wrap-by-wrap for the rest. Smoke proves no module's band is ever switched off while on screen, and that the whole band is gone (13 s) well before the road finishes merging (20 s).

**Background buildings are a vocabulary, not a box.** *"gedung-gedung yang menjadi background di sisi kiri kereta masih terlalu terlihat seperti placeholder."* `buildingAt` picks one of five types from the module hash — stepped tower with plant room and antenna, podium-and-slab with two lit crowns, three-unit shophouse row with canopy and signboard, gabled warehouse with a roller door, plain block with a rooftop water tank — over a five-tone wall palette (`M.panel`, `M.concrete`, plus `wallTan`/`wallBrick`/`wallPale`, all `shade()`d from PAL tokens and deliberately warm, since the style guide bans cold blue-black). **Combat leftovers ride the world, not the train.** *"serpihan robot masih berada di tempat dan mengikuti pergerakan kereta player."* Same root cause as the arrival terminal in section 4h: the train is what stands still in world space. Five things here are decisions, not details:

- **The arm window is invulnerable *and announced*.** Three seconds of "my shots aren't registering" reads as a bug, not a telegraph, so the HUD says `HOSTILE LOCOMOTIVE POWERING UP — ARMOR STILL SEALED` and the roof strip blinks for exactly that long. Without both, the mechanic is indistinguishable from a broken hit test.
- **The MG shoots at a dead point; tank.js's coax does not.** `entities/tank.js` re-aims its machine gun at the player's *current* position on every round, so the counter-play is to keep moving. This one locks a spot and hoses it — the counter-play is to *leave that spot*, once, inside half a second. Copying the tank's behaviour here would silently delete the mechanic, so `loco.js` documents it at the top.
- **What is pinned is the target, not the direction vector.** The consist sways ±3.5 units while firing, so the muzzle moves and the ten direction vectors are not bit-identical. The smoke test therefore measures the perpendicular distance from the locked point to each bullet's ray (max 0.000) instead of comparing directions — that is what "straight line to the same point" actually means.
- **The grenade radius is read, not copied.** The user asked for parity with the tank's mortar, so `locoBlastRadius()` evaluates `CFG.grenade.killRadius × CFG.campaign.bosses.tank.mortarBlastRatio`. Copying 0.35 into the Stage 5 block would let the two drift apart the first time the tank is retuned; smoke asserts the equality.
- **Bullet hits are a swept segment against the loco's box.** A rifle round travels tens of units per frame and the locomotive body is 24 wide — a per-frame point test tunnels straight through it. The box test clips the previous→current segment against the hull's AABB, the same reasoning as the project's bullet-vs-robot sweep.

Two build-side rules fall out of the geometry: the weapon rigs are attached to the loco **car group, not the welded hull** (they rotate, and `mergeObjectInPlace` would fuse them), and the warning strip is outside the hull too because it toggles `visible`. The grenade pool is six preallocated meshes parented to `worldRoot`, so their flight is in world coordinates rather than the moving consist's.

Arrival still gates on `etConsistDone()`, which now means *the locomotive is dead too* — the mini boss is the last thing between the player and Bandung.

#### The crew is aboard from the start (2026-08-09, user request)

Robots used to be spawned by `armCar` at the instant their car's ramp began to fall, stowed at the back of the hold, and then walk out over `emergeSec`. By the time they were combat-ready the door had been open for a while, which read as the car being empty and *then* filling up.

Now `launchEnemyConsist` loads the crew of all ten cars at once (`spawnConsistRobots`), placing each robot directly in its firing slot. Three things follow from that, and each is a rule:

- **`z.invuln` is a general flag, not a stage-5 detail.** It means "exists in the world, cannot be damaged yet", and it is honoured in *every* damage path: the bullet sweep in `robots.js`, `explodeAt` in `effects.js`, and `doMeleeHit` in `weapons.js`. Returning `{skip:true}` from a scene's `robotAI` is **not** enough — `explodeAt` walks the `robots` array itself, so without the flag a single grenade lobbed at the consist would empty three sealed cars, and those cars would then detach the moment they were armed.
- **The ramp — not the spawn — is what arms them.** `enemyTrainRobotAI` gates in three steps: not this car's turn → hidden, `invuln`, `skip`; ramp still falling → visible past `revealAtRamp` and standing ready, but still `invuln` with `aiming` false; ramp landed → `invuln` drops and the whole line opens fire together. A half-open door is not a legitimate target in either direction.
- **Sealed crew must not leak into the UI.** `countStageRobots(5)` now counts nine cars the player cannot see or touch, so the journey HUD reads `countLiveHostiles()` instead, and the radar skips any robot whose mesh the scene has hidden. The radar maps the field, not the inside of a steel box.

The cost is one allocation spike: ~30–60 robot rigs are built in a single frame instead of 3–6 at a time. It lands during `overtake`, while the consist is still off-screen behind the player and nothing else is happening, which is the cheapest moment in the journey. If it ever shows, the fix is to stagger `spawnConsistRobots` one car per frame across the overtake — not to go back to spawning on door-open.

> "daripada bikin beberapa kereta musuh datang bergantian … buat agar tiba-tiba datang kereta lain di rel sebelah … kereta sebelah ini akan langsung mendahului kereta player hingga gerbong player langsung head-to-head dengan gerbong terakhir kereta musuh ini … gerbong akan terbuka satu persatu … jika robot di gerbong itu sudah habis, gerbong itu akan meledak, terlepas, dan tertinggal kereta."

The old design sent `waveCount` short consists one after another. It is replaced by **exactly one consist of `ET_CARGO_CARS` = 10 armoured boxcars plus a shielded locomotive**, fought one car at a time:

| Mode | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| `idle` | The parallel track is empty for `consistDelaySec` (14 s) of `ride`, measured by `journey.gapT`. |
| `overtake` | The whole consist enters `entryMargin` units behind the player, **entirely behind him**, and **overtakes** over `overtakeSec` (11 s) on a smoothstep, ending with **car 0 — the rearmost — level with the player's car**. **The crew of all ten cars is loaded here, at launch** (2026-08-09, user request) — every car is sealed, no robot is visible, and every one of them is `invuln`. |

| `engage` | Both trains run at the same speed, so the consist is at relative rest with a slight sway. | `finale` | After the tenth car, the locomotive itself burns and falls behind over `finaleSec`, the group is hidden, and `etConsistDone()` becomes true. |

Car geometry keeps `enemyCarOffsetX(i) = i * ET_STEP` with **car 0 rearmost**, so `carAlignX(i) = TRAIN_BASE_X − i·ET_STEP` — aligning a later car is literally the consist braking one car-length, which is why `advance` moves in −X. Wrecks stay children of the consist group with their own `dx` drift; past `wreckHideDist` they are hidden and their ramp reset, so no dead car is ever recorded as "open".

The B > A guarantee is a hard floor in `enemyCarMix(n)`: `a = min(floor((n-1)/2), round(n * classARatio))`, so B outnumbers A for every legal `n` no matter how `classARatio` is retuned. There is no class C and no boss on the journey.

The robots are `mounted` entities using the same pattern as Stage 8's pickup riders: `separateRobots` skips them, their bodies do not push the player, `enemyTrainRobotAI` pins them to their deck slot and returns `chaseDist` purely as a firing gate, and `clampRobot` re-snaps them. **They never chase and never cross to the player's car.** Only the *active* car's robots are ever visible — every other car's are hidden behind their sealed ramp. Their firing range is the stage-level `fireRangeMeters` rather than the class chase radius, because a firing line on a parallel train is not a close-quarters brawl. Their loot is pulled into the player's car by `clampDropPos`, and both in-train sub-scenes return `false` from `bulletBlocked`/`blastBlocked` — the CSV station is long gone and cross-track fire must always connect. Every destroyed car drops ammo (plus a medkit every `medkitEveryCars`) inside the player's car.

#### The consist is built to look menacing

Each cargo car is a **sealed steel crate**, not the open flatbed of the older design (`buildEnemyCargoCar` in `props.js`): full-height far wall, full-height end bulkheads, four corner posts, a half-width armoured roof over the far side, external stiffener ribs, hazard chevrons and a teal scanner strip. The locomotive carries a four-sided **ram plough**, an armoured visor slit, three exhaust stacks, a sensor mast and hazard-striped buffers.

Three geometry rules are forced by the oblique camera (the projected sight line rises ~1.16 units per unit of ground distance, and the enemy track is 42 units *farther* than the player):

1. The **fixed** part of the near wall must stay at chest height (`ET_CAR_SILL` 8). Anything taller than ~16 units at the near wall hides the deck, which is exactly why the *closing* part is the ramp: sealed, the near wall reaches `ET_CAR_HEIGHT` 26 and the interior is genuinely invisible.
2. The roof covers only the **far 58%** of the deck. A full-width roof clips the heads of robots standing on the firing line.
3. The ramp stops at `ET_RAMP_OPEN` (0.85 rad ≈ 49°) so its tip never reaches the player's car across the 42-unit track gap.

Only the ramp, the warning strip and four near-side wheels per car stay separate meshes; each car's static hull and ramp are welded with `mergeObjectInPlace`, the same reasoning as `CombatGunship` — a single hero asset may be complex to *write* as long as it stays cheap to *draw*.

#### The parallel highway (2026-08-08, user request)

> "ketika gerbong sudah sampai yang ke-5 akan ada jalan raya yang muncul di sebelah kanan kereta player … setiap beberapa saat akan muncul mobil pickup yang berisi robot dan harus juga dihancurkan. TAPI INGAT!!! JANGAN BUAT JALAN TIBA-TIBA MUNCUL!! BUAT SEOLAH-OLAH JALAN REL KERETA MENDEKAT KE JALAN SECARA WAJAR!!"

When car `highway.fromCarIndex` (4 → the **5th** car) is armed, a highway on the **right of the train** (+z; travel is +X so right is +z, and the oblique camera puts it at the bottom of the screen, opposite the enemy track) begins to converge with the railway. Its pool (`buildJourneyHighway` in `entities/train.js`, 16 scrolling modules) is preallocated at world build and is its own group, so the `TrainSceneryPool` cap still measures the landscape alone.

**The road never pops in.** It is not one global lateral offset being animated — that reads as a slab of asphalt sliding sideways. Instead `roadOffsetAt(worldX)` in `stage5/highway.js` computes the lateral distance from each module's own **travel coordinate**:

```
s      = (travel − travelWhenArmed) + (worldX − TRAIN_BASE_X)
offset = farZ + (nearZ − farZ) · smoothstep(clamp(s / mergeDistance))
```

Because `s` includes the module's lead distance, at any instant the modules **ahead of the player are already nearer than the ones behind** — what the player sees is a road angling in and merging with the line, not a plane being translated. `mergeDistance = approachSec × trainSpeed` is measured in travel units, so the curve keeps its shape if the train speed is retuned. And the whole far end of the curve lives outside the camera: the default rig only sees to **z ≈ +118**, while `farZ` is 300, so the road genuinely arrives from off-screen. Smoke asserts all three properties — the offset is monotone, never jumps into view in a single frame, and `offsetAhead < offsetBehind` while merging.

**The road must also *read* as one continuous curve.** The first version only moved each module's `position.z`, which drew a jagged staircase (user report: *"kenapa jalannya patah-patah?"*) — every 84-unit asphalt bar stayed axis-aligned while the road's axis drifted ~9 units sideways between neighbours, so the seams stepped instead of bending. `updateJourneyHighway` now also **rotates each module onto the curve's tangent and stretches it along the arc**:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `m` | local lateral slope `dz/dx` at the module centre (central difference over `HW_SLOPE_H`) |
| `rotation.y` | `-atan(m)` — three.js maps local `+x` to world `(cos, −sin)` |
| `scale.x` | `√(1 + m²)` — the **arc** length between centres, not its x projection |

With both applied, module *i*'s far end and module *i+1*'s near end both land at `(cx + L/2, cz + L·m/2)` to first order, so the joins close: measured seam error is **0.17 units** at the steepest point. The asphalt slab is therefore exactly `L` long (an overlap would z-fight two coplanar tops); the **shoulder** is `L + 6` and sits lower, so it — not the void — is what shows through any hairline seam. `farZ` 200 / `approachSec` 20 keep the peak merge angle at **6.4°**, a plausible highway merge rather than a hard kink. Smoke measures the yaw, the arc scale and the worst seam gap from the pool's **live transforms**, so losing any of the three brings the staircase back as a failure.

Guardrails and lamp posts sit on the road's **rail-facing** edge, i.e. the side away from the camera, so nothing on the highway can ever occlude the vehicles on it.

Once the road has fully merged (`roadMerged()`), armed pickups start overtaking on it — the same `entities/enemyPickup.js` carrier Stage 8 uses, three class A/B riders each, `mounted` on the same contract (never chasing, never crossing, `chaseDist` purely a firing gate, loot pulled into the player's car by `clampDropPos`). They enter from behind, outside `groundViewExtents`, and ease to a combat offset. `nearZ` 62 and the combat offsets are chosen so every rider stays inside a class-A bullet's `bulletSpeed × rangedBulletLife` reach — the same trap that dictated `JOURNEY_TRACK_DZ`. Killing all three riders makes the carrier explode and fall behind, and drops ammo (plus a medkit every `medkitEveryPickups`) inside the player's car. The spawner stops as soon as the consist is destroyed; whatever is still on the road must still be killed before Bandung opens. Entering the arrival sub-scene calls `stopHighway()` **while the sub-scene curtain is already black**, so the road is never seen vanishing.

The dialogue script is unchanged: `breach`/`breachReply` fire as the consist overtakes, `roofWarning`/`roofReply` at the middle car, `finalApproach`/`finalReply` at the last one.

### Night mood and the Bandung city backdrop (2026-08-10, user request)

Two complaints drove this pass: *"buat agar suasananya lebih terasa malam, ini masih terlalu terang"* and *"beri banyak bangunan seperti gedung, rumah, toko, sekolah, taman... INI ADALAH KOTA BANDUNG, PUSAT KOTA BANDUNG"*.

Three material/lighting fixes finish the job. The 14 lamp PointLights keep their config-driven count but their **range drops from 125 m to 30 m** and their intensity rises: a 125 m lamp is a second ambient light — it flattens the whole deck *and* the city below it, while 30 m still covers the full deck width (the deck edge is ~112 units from a lamp head) yet stops short of the city's front row at ~213 units, so pools of warm light alternate with darkness. **The city.** `stage7/stage7City.js` builds central Bandung on the ground surface (`L.lowerY`) for the full 1.5 km on both sides: rows of two/three-storey **ruko** with shutters, awnings, lit signboards, roof decks and water tanks; **kampung** blocks of tile-roofed houses along narrow lanes; **pasar** stalls under red-and-white canopies; **sekolah** with classroom window bands, a covered walkway, a parade ground and a red-white flag; **taman** with grass, paths, benches, gazebo and trees; mid-rise **gedung** with per-floor window bands, rooftop plant and antennas. Everything is pure decor: no blocker, no nav cell, no PointLight; the districts are reported only through non-solid `recordProp` entries (`lower-city-building` / `bandung-park` / `bandung-landmark`).

Three rules shape it, and each is smoke-tested:

1. **This is a top-down game, so the backdrop is ground, not sky.** The top edge of the screen is the *farthest ground*; anything tall leaves the frame instead of forming a skyline. Measured with the stage's own projection (`stage7RobotInView`), the height budget on the −z side runs about `z −160 → y +24`, `z −250 → y −12`, `z −350 → y −48`, `z −450 → y −78`, with the city floor at y −84. So the front row (|z| 157-297) gets the detail and 4-5 storeys, the second row (353-479) reads as roofs and ground floors, and nothing beyond |z| ≈ 700 is ever visible — the exact mistake the Stage 5 journey pool made. There is deliberately **no distant skyline and no mountain ridge**; the third row exists only within 75 m of meter 700, where the landmark camera pulls back far enough to see it. A smoke assert sweeps every district through that projection and fails if one is blind.
2. **The +z side is the camera side.** The eye→player segment always lies in z 0…+70 and y 11…127, so anything beyond the deck edge whose top stays *below* the deck surface can never occlude the player. The +z row is therefore capped at `nearTopY` (−8.4) — the cap reserves headroom for roofs, parapets, tanks and antennas, not just the wall box — and is only 9 m deep, because past z ≈ 215 it sits behind the camera. A mesh-level smoke sweep proves no city mesh enters the flyover corridor or rises through the deck on that side.
3. **Deterministic, and welded.** The city is built during loading alongside every other campaign world, so layout comes from a hash of the district index and never `Math.random()` — consuming the global RNG there shifts other stages' random placement. Content is welded per **125 m chunk** with `addMergedStatic`, so ~4,200 raw meshes cost ~176 draw groups that still frustum-cull; the draw-group count, not the raw mesh count, is the guarded number.

Only when all three rider entities are dead does the pickup enter its visual explosion/wreck state and increment the destroyed count. Every third destroyed carrier spawns current-weapon ammo at the GRD LTV-45. Spawning stops at `groundPickupTarget`; the boss approach is locked until all 20 carriers have actually entered wreck state, then observes `bossApproachDelaySec` before the gunship intro.

`combatGunship.js` is a standalone procedural boss and deliberately never imports `helicopter.js`. Its **shape was totally reworked on 2026-08-08** at the user's request ("lebih bagus, lebih dramatis, lebih futuristis, lebih cinematic, lebih keren"). The old rig read as a generic helicopter — a scaled sphere for a body, four bare blades, a box tail boom, a small ball turret — which is not a silhouette for a final boss. The current rig is a faceted-plate hull with an angular tandem canopy, a six-sided nose, a gimballed chin turret carrying a four-barrel gatling plus a cannon, anhedral stub wings with pylons and three-tube missile pods, twin dorsal engine nacelles with lit exhausts, a **shrouded (ducted) five-blade main rotor** inside a full torus duct on four struts, and a **twin-boom tail with canted fins and a fenestron**. The nose deliberately points **-X**: the boss hovers *ahead* of the player at `bossX = PLAYER_X + 130` and fires toward -X, so facing -X means it is genuinely staring the player down, and the intro approach (x decreasing) becomes flying forward rather than reversing.

Three project rules shaped it. The oblique top-down camera means detail is stacked on the *upper* surfaces and the silhouette is wide rather than tall. No PointLight may be added, so every "light" is an animated emissive/Basic material. And the **static hull is welded with `mergeObjectInPlace` at build time**: the 82 authored meshes collapse to roughly eight draw calls in the browser, so the far richer boss is *cheaper* to draw than the old one — only the parts that actually move (rotor, fenestron, turret, gatling, sensor gimbal, exhausts, muzzle flash) stay standalone. `MESH_CAP.CombatGunship` was raised 60 → 95 for the same reason `Helicopter` is 70: a single hero asset that is only on screen during its own fight, and the cap measures authored complexity rather than drawn cost.

The rework also added animation, all of it **visual only — no gameplay number, timing, hitbox or spawn position changed**: the airframe banks, yaws and pitches with its hover drift instead of sliding sideways like a board; the turret and sensor gimbal track the player continuously, so the telegraph is readable from the barrels swinging onto your lane; the gatling spins up only on its own attack and coasts down afterwards; exhaust and rotor disc breathe with the attack phase; the hazard strips ignite once HP crosses the same `enrageHpFrac` that already shortens the attack gap; and the death sequence extinguishes rotor, exhaust, strips and sensor so the crash reads as an engine dying. The telegraph corridor now uses a hazard-chevron texture and PAL tokens (it previously used a raw `0xff6b35` hex), and the missile and shell meshes were rebuilt with canards, tail fins and hot nozzles. Its HP and score are its own keys under `CFG.campaign.bosses.gunship` (since 2026-08-09 — it no longer live-reads the tank's). It lives outside `robots`, performs its own swept player-bullet/direct-explosive hit tests, and cycles sequential non-overlapping attacks: 0.75-second corridor telegraph then 16 straight MG rounds; 1.1-second warning then a straight cannon shell with configured blast; one-second lock then exactly three limited-turn homing missiles 0.55 seconds apart. Missiles have configured HP and can be shot or lane-dodged. All seven corridors—including the median—are targetable. Below 50% HP the gap is multiplied by `enrageGapMul` and the barrel haulers arrive.

#### The attack cycle asks the player to read it (2026-08-19, user request)

> *"di stage 8 gimana ya kira-kira agar gunship lebih menantang tapi tidak terlalu susah"*

The honest answer was that the duel was not hard, it was **long and predictable** — so the fix raises decision density and touches no damage, HP or speed number. Two changes, and each fixes a distinct reason the fight could be played on autopilot.

**The attack order was a fixed rotation.** `endAttack` advanced `(attackIdx + 1) % 3`, so it was MG → CANNON → MISSILE forever; after one cycle (~15 s) the player knew every attack in advance and the telegraph — which the 2026-08-08 rework had gone to some trouble to make readable, with the turret swinging onto your lane and the gatling spinning up — was decoration. The order is now drawn from a **shuffle bag** rather than pure randomness, and that choice is the point: pure randomness can deal three missile bursts in a row (unfair) or hide the cannon for fifteen seconds (dull), while a bag guarantees **every three attacks contain each type exactly once** with the order inside those three unpredictable. The bag boundary swaps its tail when it would repeat the type just used, so two identical attacks never land back to back. **The opener stays MG on purpose** — the least punishing attack teaches the telegraph before the cannon and missiles join in — and `resetCombatGunship` seeds `lastAttack` with it so the first random pick cannot immediately repeat it.

**Two of the three attacks were answered by the same input.** `startTelegraph` locked `targetLane` to the player's current lane, for the cannon as well as the MG, so both were solved identically: watch the marker, leave that lane. Only the missiles asked for something else. The cannon now **leads the player's lateral movement** — its target is `laneOf(playerZ + clamp(playerVZ × cannonLeadSec, ±laneWidth × cannonLeadMaxLanes))`, and the marker keeps tracking that prediction every telegraph frame before locking at the moment of fire, so the marker visibly sliding ahead of you *is* the tell. The counter-play inverts: standing still means it lands on you and you must move, while sliding means it lands ahead of you and you must **stop or reverse**. The MG deliberately keeps its start-of-telegraph lock — its corridor is a promise that must not move — so the three attacks now have three different answers.

Three constraints kept this from becoming "harder" rather than "more challenging". The impact still **snaps to a lane centre**, honouring the Stage 8 invariant that projectiles commit to lane centres so telegraphs stay readable. `cannonLeadSec` (0.9) is deliberately **shorter than the shell's ~1.04 s flight**, so the prediction always errs in the player's favour. And the lane width the lead is measured against is derived from the caller's own `ctx.laneZ`, not read from `CFG.campaign.stage8`, so the boss module stays stage-agnostic; a caller that supplies neither `playerVZ` nor `laneOf` transparently gets the old lane-lock behaviour. **HP stayed at 9000 at the user's request**, so the duel is the same length — only denser.

At zero HP every gunship projectile is cleared and boss music stops. Rotor speed decays while the body rolls, burns and falls away; only after `deathDone` does the configured three-second post-crash delay run. The GRD LTV-45 and highway continue moving through the duel and death. Arrival then swaps through a 0.5-second fade to static Kertajati, plays the exact `STAGE8_DIALOGUE` arrival lines, and normal/skip cleanup converge on green `STAGE 8 COMPLETE` with Total Time, Loot Boxes and checkpoint 8 preserved. Since 2026-08-13 Stage 8 is no longer the endpoint: `CONTINUE` opens the Field Shop and `Start Next Stage` enters Stage 9, exactly like every other Stage 1–12 finish.

### Background — Bandung city → West Java rice fields (2026-08-17, user request)

> *"perbaiki background di Stage 8. buat agar backgroundnya di perkotaan kota Bandung. kemudian ketika hampir melawan boss, transisikan backgroundnya menjadi di persawahan khas Jawa Barat. dan ketika melawan boss, backgroundnya tetap di persawahan khas Jawa Barat."*

Everything outside the carriageways now belongs to `stages/stage8/scenery.js`. What it replaced was three generic prop rows repeated by `index % 3` inside the road module — two trees, two concrete sheds, two grey cones — plus two rice patches at indices 8 and 11; and **outside the shoulder (|z| > 74) there was no ground surface at all**, so what showed under them was `scene.background`, the city haze. That is the same failure the Stage 5 landscape had before 2026-08-09.

**Two acts, fully preallocated.** Every module carries *both* landscapes as child groups (`cityG`/`riceG`) and the act only toggles `visible`. No mesh, material or PointLight is ever born when the landscape changes, so the no-mid-game-shader-recompile invariant holds. Each variant is welded separately with `mergeObjectInPlace`, so 3,352 raw meshes cost 453 draw groups of which only **297 are drawn** at a time (one act plus the act-neutral strip), and frustum culling removes the modules that are off screen on top of that.

**The act changes by travelling, never in front of the player.** The stage only sets a *target* act; a module may adopt it only while it is off screen — either through `wrap()` (reborn far ahead of the player) or through the one-shot `relayoutAhead()` at the threshold, which re-dresses everything already parked beyond `S8_SCENERY_AHEAD` (900 — outside the camera footprint's `maxX`, smoke-derived from `groundViewExtents` rather than hardcoded). Without the relayout the new act could only arrive through wrap, and one rotation of the far pool takes ~54 s. The nearest relaid modules take a dither pattern so the city reads as *thinning out* into paddies instead of stopping at a straight line; the far horizon is deliberately **not** dithered, because a silhouette has to move as one line. Smoke audits every tick of the whole stage and fails if a single module ever swaps act while on screen.

**Threshold.** `CFG.campaign.stage8.scenery.riceAfterFraction` (0.65) is read against `pickupsDestroyed / groundPickupTarget`, so the change is driven by *pursuit progress*, not by a timer — it lands with roughly seven carriers still to kill, and by `bossApproach` the transition has finished. From `bossApproach` onward (`gunshipIntro`, `gunshipBattle`, `gunshipDeath`, `arrival`) the target is unconditionally `rice`, so the duel is always fought over the paddies and can never fall back to the city. The target is evaluated once at the *end* of the frame, after `updatePickups` has booked the kill — evaluating it inside `updateRoad` (which runs first) made the carrier that crosses the threshold register a frame late.

**Three pools, three parallaxes**, all spanning 1680 units so one wrap constant serves all of them: `near` 1.0 (it touches the ground), `mid` 0.62, `far` 0.34. Without parallax the whole backdrop slides in lockstep and immediately reads as one flat slab. A fourth act-neutral `base` pool rides at parallax 1.0 with the near pool and carries the ground slab, verge, ditch and delineators — so there can never be a hole in the ground during a swap.

**The layout is dictated by the top-down camera**, exactly as in Stage 7's city. Stage 8's gameplay camera sits at (−134.4 / +127.2 / +122.4); measured from that projection the visible height budget is roughly 95 units at z −120, 80 at −200, 65 at −300, 45 at −420 and 15 at −600, and the *camera* side (+z) runs out at z ≈ 155. So the **tallest buildings are in the front row** (z −110…−175), the mid row is mid-rise, and the far horizon is deliberately clipped by the top edge of the frame — that clipping is what fills it. Nothing is built outside the footprint, and smoke proves it from `groundViewExtents` with the stage's own `camOffset` rather than a hardcoded number.

**The +z band frames the bottom of the screen.** The eye→player sight line is already at y ≈ 52 where it crosses z 86 (with the player in the outermost right lane) and climbs steeply beyond that, so the foreground band's 40-unit-tall shophouses and coconut palms cannot occlude the player, the carriers or the gunship. It is deliberately gapped so it never becomes a tunnel.

**City act:** a service road with kerbs, then four lots per module drawn from five recipes (three-unit *ruko* with lit shopfronts and a continuous awning, kampung houses with clay hip roofs and yard walls, office/apartment blocks with setbacks, roof tanks and window bands, gable-roofed workshops with roller doors, and a columned market shed), a second row of mid-rise silhouettes behind them, street lamps, parked cars including the green *angkot*, street trees, a power line whose cables span exactly one module so neighbours join up, and a bus shelter or a water tower on a hash of the module index. `mid` adds dense mid-rise blocks plus a sawtooth-roofed factory with chimneys; `far` adds a clipped skyline with sparse lit strips.

**Rice act:** an irrigation canal along the foot of the road, then three rows of bunded paddy plots stepping up as they recede, each plot alternating open water and young rice by hash, a stilted *saung*, coconut palms, banana clumps, bamboo, haystacks and clusters of genteng-roofed kampung houses. `mid` climbs into four terrace steps with tree clumps, bamboo groves and a village with a domed mosque and minaret; `far` is the Priangan ridge — large cones whose peaks are cut off by the frame edge, with a twin peak on alternating modules and a foothill tree line.

**Two follow-up fixes (2026-08-17, same session).** *"perbaiki bentuk pohon-pohonnya. sepertinya ada pohon yang bentuk daunnya terbalik. di sisi kanan jalan masih terlihat area biru yang kosong."*

1. **No landscape mesh stands inverted.** The coconut crown was a cone flipped with `rotation.x = PI`; from an oblique camera that reads exactly as a downward-pointing triangle, not as fronds. The crown is now six real fronds — flat blades that extend outward from the top of the trunk and droop (`blade()`, which yaws *and* tilts in one Euler so the blade points where it is offset) — plus a nut cluster. The banana plant had a related bug: its leaves were offset toward `(cos a, sin a)` but yawed by `a`, and yaw maps local +x to `(cos a, −sin a)`, so every leaf jutted one way while facing another; both now use `−a` and droop the same way. Smoke rejects any landscape mesh with |rotation.x| or |rotation.z| ≥ π/2.

2. **The ground now runs past the frame edge instead of stopping at it.** The first pass ended the ground at z +155 and −520, but the camera footprint measured on the landscape's own ground plane (y −0.9) reaches +160/−718 at 16:9 and +187/−838 at 21:9. The result was a band of `scene.background` haze along the **bottom-right** edge — precisely where world scale is largest, so a handful of world units became a fat blue strip — plus a bare wedge at the foot of the horizon. The camera-side ground now runs to +205 and the backdrop ground to −860 (one box per module each, covering 21:9 with margin), the slabs tuck **under** the road shoulder at `GROUND_IN` 66 so there is no seam between asphalt and terrain, and the newly opened depth is dressed: the foreground band gains a back yard with a fence and trees (city) or a third paddy with a bund and a palm (rice), and a low horizon-foot row at z ≈ −560 (clipped to ~20 visible units) keeps the deepest ground from reading as an empty plate. **Smoke now asserts ground *coverage*** — the union of ground z-spans must cover the entire footprint plus 15% headroom in **both** acts — rather than only checking that props sit inside it, which is what let the gap through. Raw meshes rose 3,352 → 3,895 while the draw cost stayed at 453 welded / 297 drawn, because the additions reuse existing material buckets.

Layout uses a **deterministic hash of the module index, never `Math.random()`** — the pool is built during loading alongside every other campaign world, and consuming the global RNG there shifts unrelated stages' random placement. The same pass removed the two `rand()` calls the old road module made at build time. The landscape is **pure decor**: zero blockers, zero nav cells, zero PointLights (the stage's light count stays exactly 12), no place-name/shop-name/wayfinding boards, and every material is a PAL token with emissive ≤ `EMISSIVE_MAX`.

### Second enemy — N.U.S.A. VULTURE-B barrel hauler (2026-08-17, user request)

> *"tambahkan musuh baru. mobil pickup yang akan menurunkan barrel ... di lajur mobil player berada. jadi player harus menghindarinya ke kiri atau ke kanan. mobil pickup ini akan selalu muncul dari depan. barel yang diturunkan ini memiliki HP 150. mobil pickupnya sendiri memiliki HP 230. munculkan 1 setiap setelah 5 mobil pickup robot yang muncul."*

`stages/stage8/barrelDropper.js` adds the stage's second enemy type. It is deliberately a different *kind* of threat from the Raven-K carrier: it has no riders, never shoots, never chases, and is not part of the `groundPickupTarget` gate. It attacks through the player's **position**, which is what finally gives A/D pressure of its own in a stage where lane changes had only ever been a way to line up a shot.

**Cadence.** One hauler for every `CFG.campaign.stage8.barrelDropper.everyPickups` (5) robot carriers that *spawn* — counted on spawn rather than on kill, so it cannot be avoided by stalling the fight. With `groundPickupTarget` at 20 that is four haulers per run.

**Entry.** Always from the front, born outside `groundViewExtents` at the forward end of the road pool (exactly the rule the Raven-K front entries already use), then easing to `leadOffset` (150 units) ahead of the player where it holds station. It *must* be ahead: a dropped barrel immediately sits still on the asphalt, so it sweeps backward into the player at precisely ground speed. Reaction time is `leadOffset / roadSpeed` ≈ 1.6 s against a 0.32 s lane change, and smoke asserts that ratio from config rather than from a fixed number.

**The truck chases your lane.** `t.lane` follows the player's lane every frame and the chassis slides across to it; a barrel is only released once the truck is genuinely aligned (`ALIGN_TOL`, 0.6 of a lane). This is not cosmetic — a truck that held its spawn lane while its barrel appeared in the player's lane would look like the barrel had spawned in mid-air beside it, and the truck's slide into your lane is the real telegraph, arriving well before the tailgate swings open. Because it re-targets continuously, dodging once is not enough; the next drum follows you. The impact point is snapped to the **lane centre**, never the player's raw z, since a barrel landing between two lanes could not be dodged in either direction.

**A dropped barrel is a real barrel.** It is pushed into the shared `barrels` array from `entities/barrels.js`, so it inherits every already-tested behaviour: the anti-tunnel swept bullet test in `barrelBulletHits()`, the explosion through `detonateBarrel` (queued via `queueBoom`, so there is no reentrant splice), chain reactions, player and robot damage, debris and dust. Only two things are Stage 8's own — its HP (`barrelHp` 150, instead of `CFG.barrels.hp`) and its motion. Its **mesh still comes from a preallocated pool**, so the stage never births a mesh mid-run; because `detonateBarrel` calls `scene.remove()` and these meshes belong to the stage world root, the rig releases them from their own parent instead.

**It only detonates if the lane still matches.** The barrel explodes when it crosses the player's x *and* is still within half a lane plus the barrel radius. A barrel the player dodged rolls past inertly — if it detonated anyway, the 6 m blast would reach the neighbouring lane and make the manoeuvre pointless.

**Rolling axes (2026-08-17, user report: *"barrel menggelinding dengan posisi poros yang salah. ban mobilnya juga berputar dengan posisi poros yang salah."*).** Both were genuine axis errors, and both come from the same fact: a three.js `CylinderGeometry`'s axis is **+Y**.

- **The barrel** was left standing upright and spun on `rotation.z`, which tips an upright drum over sideways rather than rolling it. Each pooled barrel is now wrapped in a **pivot**: the barrel mesh is offset by −`BARREL_HEIGHT`/2 so its centre sits at the pivot origin, `pivot.rotation.x = π/2` lays it **across** the road (its own +Y axis falls onto world +z), and `pivot.rotation.y` then spins it about that axis — under Euler `XYZ` the y-component's world axis is `Rx·(0,1,0)` = +z, which is the only correct roll axis for something travelling along x. The pivot sits at `y = BARREL_RADIUS` so the drum rests on the asphalt instead of sinking into it, and the sign is positive because the barrel travels toward −x. Smoke pins the lay-down angle, the absence of any sideways tip, and rolling *without slipping* (`dθ = dx / R`).
- **The wheels** were spun on `rotation.x` — the direction of travel — so the tyres turned like a coin spinning on its edge. They now follow the convention `enemyPickup.js` and `tacticalVehicle.js` already used: the axle is baked into the **geometry** (`geo.rotateX(π/2)`), leaving the object's own rotation free, and rolling is `rotation.z = −wheelPhase` accumulated from the road speed (the truck holds station relative to the player, but physically it is travelling at ground speed, and that is the rate the tyres must read). Any new wheeled prop in this project follows the same convention.

**The tailgate runs one full cycle per barrel (2026-08-18, user request: *"buat agar pintu belakang mobil itu terbuka kemudian barel menggelinding jatuh kemudian pintu tertutup"*).** The drop used to be a single frame: the barrel was placed on the asphalt behind the truck while the tailgate, which had ramped open as a telegraph, snapped shut on that same frame. Nothing was ever seen *leaving* the door. It is now a three-stage cycle per barrel, and `runGate()` prioritises the stages from the last backwards so a closing door is never overwritten by the next barrel's telegraph:

1. **Open** over `dropTelegraphSec` (0.40) as the warning.
2. **Hold** wide open for `dropFallSec` (0.34) while the drum rolls down off the lip.
3. **Close** over `dropCloseSec` (0.30) — and the truck's last barrel keeps its cycle even after the truck switches to `depart`.

The barrel itself is born at the mesh's **`dropAnchor`** — the tailgate lip's own world offset, derived from the hinge position and the rig's scale, not a guessed point behind the truck. It starts **half-upright and airborne** (`rotation.x = pi/2 - DROP_TIP`) and lies flat exactly as it touches the asphalt, falling on a gravity curve with one small bounce. Its backward sweep ramps from 0.3x to full ground speed across the fall: released at full speed it would be visibly yanked backward the instant it appeared, since the truck holds station while the road scrolls. The roll is taken from the barrel's **real displacement** rather than from the ground speed, so rolling-without-slipping stays exactly true while it is still slowing down in the air.

The load-bearing consequence: **`dropTelegraphSec + dropFallSec + dropCloseSec` must fit inside `dropGapSec`** (1.04 <= 1.10 as tuned). Smoke asserts that relation directly *and* measures, across a full six-barrel unload, that the door's opening returns to zero between every pair of barrels.

**Payload density (2026-08-18, user request: *"buat agar barrel yang dijatuhkannya lebih banyak dengan interval waktu yang lebih singkat"*).** `dropCount` went 3 → **6** and `dropGapSec` 2.2 → **1.1**, so a single hauler now lays a genuine minefield down the lane instead of three spaced drums. Three things follow from that, and they are the reason this is not a one-line config edit:

- **Each barrel keeps its full reaction time.** That window is `leadOffset / roadSpeed` ≈ 1.6 s and has nothing to do with the gap between drops, so a shorter interval raises *density* — several barrels in the air at once, in different lanes as the truck re-chases you — without ever producing a barrel that cannot be dodged. Smoke still asserts the reaction-time ratio from config.
- **`dropTelegraphSec` had to shrink with it** (0.7 → **0.45**). The tailgate opening is the drop's warning; at a 1.1 s gap a 0.7 s telegraph would leave it hanging open almost permanently and it would stop reading as a signal at all. Smoke now requires `dropTelegraphSec < dropGapSec`.
- **The bed's drum count is derived, not authored.** The visible drums *are* the remaining payload, so a hardcoded three would have made a six-barrel truck look empty from its second drop onward. The cargo is now laid out from `dropCount` up to the bed's physical capacity of `BARREL_DROPPER_CARGO_MAX` (3 along the bed × 2 across it — stacking is blocked by the gantry at y 3.50), and the barrel **pool size is derived from `maxActive × dropCount`** as well: a barrel lives from `leadOffset` all the way past the player, which is longer than the whole drop sequence, so a fixed pool would quietly starve and swallow drops the moment `dropCount` was raised again.

**Its arrival is not announced (2026-08-19, user request: *"hilangkan prompt info kedatangan mobil barel, player gak sebodoh itu"*).** The first hauler of a run used to queue a Vehicle System line, a Gibran reply and a tutorial banner naming the mechanic. All three are gone, script entries deleted from `config/gameplay.json` along with them. The truck already telegraphs itself three separate times before a single barrel touches the road — it enters from the front in clear view, it slides across into the player's lane, and it opens its tailgate a beat before the first drum rolls out. Narrating three visible cues is what the request objected to, and it was right. Smoke now fails if a hauler spawn queues any dialogue at all, or if a hauler-themed `showStageMsg` reappears in the stage source.

**The truck itself** has HP 230 and its own swept player-bullet test (a rifle round covers tens of units per frame, so a per-frame point test would tunnel through it — the same reasoning as the gunship and the Stage 5 locomotive). Its bed drums disappear one by one as they are dropped, so shooting it early genuinely denies the remaining barrels. On death it explodes and its wreck brakes to a stop on the road like every other Stage 8 leftover, and it pays out `barrelDropper.loot`. Zero PointLights, PAL tokens only, and the whole rig is cleared when the gunship intro begins so no ground hazard hangs over the boss cutscene. The radar plots both the hauler and its rolling barrels in red.

### Forward and back, not only left and right (2026-08-20, user request)

> *"saya ingin di stage 8, selain mobil player bisa belok kiri kanan, mobil player bisa bergerak ke depan dan ke belakang."*

`W` and `S` now drive the GRD LTV-45 up and down the road inside a window around the arena centre, alongside the free `A`/`D` steering. The control is deliberately the same shape as the steering added the day before — **held, not edge-triggered, and it stops wherever it is released** — but with its own `advanceEaseSec`, because an armoured vehicle changes its longitudinal speed far more lazily than it slides its tail out.

**`PLAYER_X` was demoted from "where the player is" to "the centre of the arena".** `currentX` is now the source of truth for the longitudinal position, exactly as `currentZ` became the source of truth for the lateral one when the lane snap was removed. That single rename is what made the change tractable — but it also split a distinction that had been invisible for as long as the player could not move:

- **Things that CHASE the player** — the barrel hauler's station and lane lock, carrier combat offsets, the gunship's `roadX`/`bossX`, loot and ammo drop positions, `clampDropPos`, cutscene framing — read the **live** `currentX`.
- **Things that are part of the WORLD** — the ends of the fixed road pool, the arena bounds in `stage8Walk`, `S8_START` — stay anchored to the **centre**.

Getting that backwards is not a subtle bug. Entry positions have to satisfy *both* constraints at once — a carrier must be born beyond the end of the road pool **and** outside the player's view — and while the player was pinned those were the same number. Pointing the road-pool end at the live player made carriers and barrel haulers spawn past the end of the asphalt as soon as the player drove forward. Smoke caught it immediately; the ctx field is now the absolute `roadMaxX` rather than a relative `roadEdge`, so the two anchors can no longer be confused by whoever reads them next.

Three things are derived rather than invented:

- **Peak speed is clamped below `roadSpeed`** (`ADVANCE_SPEED_CAP`, 0.9). If the player could fall back faster than the asphalt scrolls, the wheels would have to spin *backwards* while the entire landscape still swept rearwards. Braking must never become reversing. Measured: holding `S` takes the vehicle's ground speed from 92 down to 48.6 and no further.
- **The wheels roll at `groundSpeed()`** — `roadSpeed + advanceVel`, the speed relative to the asphalt — not at the scroll rate. Accelerating and braking are visible in the tyres, and the front-wheel steer angle uses the same figure, so the steering geometry stays honest at every speed.
- **The window is a config distance and a config crossing time** (`advanceRange` 96, `advanceSec` 2.2), and the window sits well inside the ±260 arena bound, so the road and scenery pools always cover the view.

**What it changes in play.** Because everything that pursues the player tracks the live position, driving forward does not shake off a carrier or the gunship — they keep station on you, which is what pursuit means. What *does* change is your relationship with everything already on the road: **a dropped barrel is fixed in world space and sweeps rearwards at ground speed**, so pushing forward shortens the time you have to deal with it and backing off buys more. That consequence falls out of the coordinate model rather than being scripted, and it is the reason the throttle is worth having at all.

Two things deliberately not done, to keep the change to what was asked: the avatar's inertia lurch is still fed lateral acceleration only, so braking and accelerating do not pitch Major Gibran forward and back the way a swerve rolls him; and the road scroll rate is untouched, so the world's speed is the world's, not the player's.

### Free steering replaces the lane snap (2026-08-19, user request)

> *"coba buat agar mobil player tidak snap ke kiri dan ke kanan dong. coba bikin lebih fleksibel."*

Stage 8 had shipped with edge-triggered lane snaps since it was written: one press of A or D committed the vehicle to an eased slide into the neighbouring slot, and holding the key did nothing extra. Holding A or D now **accelerates the GRD LTV-45 sideways**, and it stops wherever it is released — anywhere across the carriageway, including between two lanes.

**What made this cheap was inverting which value is authoritative.** `currentZ` — the continuous lateral position — is now the source of truth, and `laneIndex` is nothing but a read-out of it (`nearestLane(currentZ)`). Every system that asks *"which lane is the player in"* keeps working with no change at all: the gunship's MG and cannon telegraphs, the barrel hauler's lane chase, the snapping of dropped barrels to lane centres. Projectiles still commit to lane **centres**, which is what keeps the telegraphs readable now that the player no longer lives on them.

Four things are derived rather than invented, so the existing tuning keeps its meaning:

- Peak lateral speed is `laneWidth / laneChangeSec` — one lane still costs exactly what it always did.
- The median still slows you, by the existing `laneChangeSec : medianChangeSec` ratio, so grass remains worse than asphalt without a second concept.
- `steerEaseSec` is the only new config key: the accel/damping that makes the control analog instead of a teleport. Release the key and the vehicle coasts briefly to a stop rather than stopping dead.
- The vehicle **yaws toward its steer direction**, visual only, which is what makes free steering read as driving rather than sliding.

**One consequence is worth keeping rather than papering over:** clipping one lane across is no longer enough to dodge a barrel. Stopping right on the lane boundary still leaves you inside the barrel's width, so a dodge now means actually clearing it. That surfaced as a smoke failure during this change, and it is a fair skill demand rather than a bug.

**The front axle steers** (2026-08-19, follow-up request). The wheels have baked their axle into the geometry since they were built, which leaves `rotation.z` for rolling — and, as it turns out, `rotation.y` free for steering. Euler `XYZ` composes those as Ry·Rz: the wheel spins on its own axle first and the whole assembly is then yawed, which is precisely how a steered wheel behaves.

The angle is derived rather than authored. The vehicle runs forward at `roadSpeed` while sliding sideways at `steerVel`, so its true heading is `atan2(lateral, forward)` — about 0.38 rad (22°) at full lateral speed — and that is where the front wheels point. Two details are easy to get wrong, so both are smoke-guarded: the **body yaw is subtracted**, because the wheels are children of a group that is already yawed and steering would otherwise be counted twice; and the **hubs steer with their tyres**, or the rim sits straight inside a turned tyre. The mechanical limit and the steering lag live in `tacticalVehicle.js`, since how far a wheel can turn is a property of the vehicle — only the kinematics belong to the scene.

**The rider is carried, and a swerve throws him** (2026-08-19, follow-up request). Free steering moves the logic pivot sideways, and the avatar's gait is derived from pivot displacement — so the first thing free steering produced was Major Gibran walking sideways in the roof hatch. Stage 8 now calls `setAvatarCarried(true)` for the whole ride and feeds `setAvatarVehicleLean()` the vehicle's normalised lateral **acceleration**, which throws him the opposite way on a lightly overshooting spring. Details of the rig side are in [presentation.md](presentation.md#riding-a-vehicle--carried-gait-and-the-inertia-lurch-2026-08-19-user-request).

The retired machinery — `requestLane`, `laneFrom`/`laneTo`/`laneT`, the queued `laneBuffer`, the held-key edge flags — is gone rather than left dormant. Smoke was rewritten around a steering helper instead of key edges, and it now pins the three properties that define the feel: holding D travels well past one lane, releasing leaves the vehicle where it stands with **zero** subsequent drift, and the median caps steering speed lower than asphalt. Running the original snap implementation against these tests reports `1.00 lajur` for the hold and `hanyut 7.560` for the release — the snap pulling the vehicle to a lane centre after the key came up.

### The hauler arms while it flies in (2026-08-19, user report)

> *"buat agar ketika mobil barel sudah mencapai posisinya, barel akan langsung diturunkan. sekarang terasa ada jeda yang cukup lama."*

Two separate things were adding up to that pause, and only one of them was a timer.

**The arm timer waited for arrival.** `armSec` (1.2 s) and the tailgate telegraph were only counted down inside the `drop` phase, so the truck flew all the way into station, stopped, and *then* began arming with its door still shut. The countdown and the door now run during `approach` as well: the truck arrives already loaded and already opening, and the first drum leaves the lip on the next frame. It still drops nothing while approaching — the drop condition remains owned by the `drop` phase — so no barrel is ever born far up the road where the player cannot see it leave the truck.

**"Arrived" was measured with a 6-unit threshold on an exponential ease.** The approach closes the remaining distance by a fixed *fraction* per second, so the truck crawls the last stretch: it visually parks long before it is within 6 units of station, and the code kept waiting through that crawl. The threshold is now a fraction of the truck's **own length**, which is what the eye is actually judging. The general rule: an exponential approach needs its arrival threshold scaled to the size of the thing approaching, never to a small constant.

Measured dead time between parking and the first barrel: **1.3 s → 0.1 s** (one tick at the test's resolution). Smoke measures exactly that interval in a live run rather than asserting on the config, and reverting either half puts it back to 1.3 s.

### The boss duel's escort pair — one barrel hauler + one robot carrier (2026-08-19, user request)

> *"di stage 8 gunship. buat agar ketika HP gunship kurang dari 50% maka akan muncul 1 mobil barel dan 1 mobil robot. dan jika mobil barel DAN mobil robot itu hancur, maka 3 detik kemudian akan spawn lagi. jika gunship dikalahkan dan masih ada mobil itu, maka mobil itu akan langsung meledak juga. stage berakhir sukses."*

This replaces the interval-driven stationary haulers written earlier the same day. The second half of the duel is now fought against a **pair** of ground vehicles: one **N.U.S.A. VULTURE-B barrel hauler** spawned `{endless: true}` — it holds station and keeps unloading until its HP is gone, never departing — and one **Raven-K robot carrier** with its usual three mounted A/B riders. The truck has to be shot, the carrier is disabled by killing its three riders, and the gunship is overhead the whole time. Three sources of pressure, one player.

`spawnBarrelDropper`'s `{endless, maxActive}` flag is unchanged and still what **changes what the hauler is**: during the ground pursuit it is an obstacle that passes through and can simply be dodged; during the duel it is a target that must be destroyed.

Five rules hold it together:

- **The trigger is HP, never elapsed time.** `gunship.hp <= maxHp × bossEscort.hpFrac` (0.5), so the extra pressure always arrives exactly when the duel turns, regardless of how long the player took to get there. The first pair arrives after `firstDelaySec`.
- **The pair is tracked by reference, not by a repeating timer.** `bossHauler`/`bossCarrier` hold the two spawned objects and `bossEscortLive()` asks whether *either* is still alive. While one of them lives, **no countdown runs at all** — this is the whole difference from the interval version, which kept sending trucks on a clock regardless of what was already on the road. A player who shoots fast is given a breather; a player who ignores them is never buried.
- **The respawn delay starts only when BOTH are destroyed**, and it is `respawnDelaySec` (3 s) from that moment. Leaving one vehicle alive therefore holds the next wave back — a real tactical choice, and the reason the rule is worded around the pair rather than around each vehicle.
- **A pair is exactly one of each.** `BOSS_ESCORT_TRUCKS`/`BOSS_ESCORT_CARRIERS` are constants in the scene, not config, for the same reason `TRAIN_CAR_COUNT` is: they size the preallocated pools (`barrelSlotsNeeded(maxTrucks)`, the carrier slots). The truck pool keeps its slack because a **wreck still occupies its slot** for `wreckSec` while no longer counting as active, and the carrier pool is floored at one escort plus one spare for the same reason.
- **An endless truck's bed cycles rather than emptying forever.** `showCargo` uses `dropped % cargo.length` for these, so the drums still visibly deplete within each batch — the cue that makes shooting the truck feel worthwhile — while the truck plainly never runs dry.

**When the gunship dies, whatever is still on the asphalt goes up with it.** `detonateBossEscort()` runs on the same frame the boss is declared dead, and it deliberately uses each vehicle's **normal death path** — `damageBarrelDropper(t, t.hp)` for the truck, `killRobot(…, {cause:'explosion'})` for the surviving riders then `destroyPickup` for the carrier — so the explosion, the shattered wreck, the gibs and the loot are identical to killing them by hand. There is no silent-removal path: a vehicle that vanished would look like a bug at the most triumphant moment in the stage. The wrecks then brake to a stop on the road like every other Stage 8 leftover, and are recycled inside `swapToAirport` while the screen is already black. The stage still ends exactly as before — `STAGE 8 COMPLETE`, checkpoint 8 preserved.

Smoke drives the whole arc in a real duel: no vehicle may appear while the boss is above the threshold; the threshold is crossed by damage rather than by waiting; exactly one truck and one carrier (with three riders) arrive; the truck drops well past a full load without ever entering `depart`; **destroying only the truck holds the next wave** (the assert that would catch a regression back to the interval version); killing the carrier's riders starts the 3-second countdown and a second pair arrives after it and not before; and killing the boss detonates both survivors, leaving no mounted rider alive.

### The missile's hitbox has to cover the missile (2026-08-18, user report)

> *"kok sepertinya homming missile sangat susah ditembak, buat agar mudah ditembak dan dihancurkan."*

Two causes, and the first one was a side effect of the size pass earlier the same day. `missileHitRadius` had been sitting at **5** — a 10-unit target circle — since the missile was a ~8-unit stub. Making the missile readable grew its drawn body to about **31 units long**, and nothing updated the radius, so rounds that visibly passed straight through the missile registered nothing at all. That is the same failure the project already has a rule for with spawn machines and Stage 3's deploying factories: **what is drawn is what you hit.**

The radius is now **12** — a 24-unit circle, covering most of the drawn length but deliberately never more than it, since registering hits on empty air beside the missile is just as wrong as missing the body. And `missileHp` drops **80 → 40**, so one standard rifle round destroys a missile rather than two; shooting one down is now a reaction, not a burst.

Both are `config/gameplay.json` values, so they stay retunable. What smoke pins is the *relation*, in two independent ways: the built mesh is measured against the configured radius (hitbox ≥ 60% of the drawn length, ≤ 100% of it, and still smaller than the boss's own `hitRadius` — a missile is a small thing passing by, not a wall), and a real rifle-damage round is pushed through a live missile during the duel to confirm it actually dies in one game tick. Reverting the two numbers fails both, reporting `diameter 10 vs badan 31, HP 80 vs rifle 40`.

### Gunship projectiles leave their muzzles (2026-08-18, user request)

> *"buat agar peluru gunship benar-benar keluar dari moncong senjatanya. buat tiba-tiba spawn di tengah jalan. canon dan mgun berada di bagian depan gunship. homming missile dari sayapnya kiri dan kanan."*

**The anchors already existed; the fire functions simply ignored them.** The rig has carried `mgMuzzle` and `cannonMuzzle` on its front chin turret since it was built, and the wing pods have been there just as long — but `fireMG`, `fireCannon` and `fireMissile` each used an invented offset a few units from the hull origin, at y 8-13 (road level), already sitting in the **target lane** rather than at the aircraft. The result was exactly what the report says: every shot popped into existence in the middle of the road in front of the player, with no visible connection to the boss.

All three now spawn from real anchors read through `getWorldPosition` into a single module-level scratch vector — the pattern `tank.js` already uses for its own cannon and coax, so no allocation happens per shot. The wings gained `missileRails`, two `Object3D` anchors at the pod mouths; they hang off `group` rather than the hull, because the hull is welded with `mergeObjectInPlace` and anything inside it stops being addressable. Missiles alternate between the left and right rail.

Three things had to hold for this to be a purely visual improvement:

- **The MG corridor still means something.** Fire now converges: each round flies from the chin turret to the target lane's centre *at the player's own x*. The lateral offset is therefore largest at the muzzle and zero exactly where the player stands, so a burst can never rake a neighbouring lane at the point where someone is actually standing. The telegraph corridor keeps its meaning.
- **The cannon keeps its timing.** The shell's velocity is scaled so its **X component stays exactly `cannonSpeed`**, which means moving the spawn cannot silently shorten or lengthen the dodge window. The one intended change is that the nose muzzle sits about 22 units nearer the player than the old spawn point, trimming ~0.18 s from a warning that is dominated by the 1.1 s telegraph.
- **The missile dives.** It launches at wing height and eases down to `MISSILE_CRUISE_Y`, because the homing logic steers purely in XZ: a missile left at wing height would sail over the player's head while still registering a hit, since the enemy-bullet test projects to the ground plane.

Smoke pins each origin against the boss's own position rather than against fixed coordinates — MG and cannon at turret height in front of the hull, missiles at wing height with launches recorded on **both** sides. Reverting the three spawns reports `y 13 vs bos 41` for the MG and sides `1/0/-1` for the missiles, the middle one being the old centreline launch that never came from a wing at all.

### Gunship projectile readability (2026-08-18, user report)

> *"arah peluru machine gun nya masih tidak searah dengan jalan, peluru machine gun malah melintang."* — and: *"perbaiki bentuk peluru dari canon dan homming missile. buat agar lebih besar dan lebih terlihat jelas agar player menyadari kedatangannya."*

**The MG tracer was genuinely crosswise, and the cause is the same class of bug as the rolling barrel.** `GEO.bullet` is a **sphere**, so the only thing that gives a tracer its streak is `scale` — and `fireMG` scaled it `(1.15, 1.15, 7)`, i.e. along **z**, while the round flies along **−x**. Every burst therefore laid a row of crossbars across the road. The elongation axis is now rotated onto the flight direction, and the yaw is derived from the fire direction itself (`rotation.y = atan2(dir.x, dir.z)`) rather than baked in as a constant, so it stays correct if the gunship ever fires anywhere but straight down the road. Smoke asserts the *relation* — the dot product of the tracer's rotated long axis with its own `dir` must be 1 — not a hardcoded angle, which is what makes it a real guard rather than a copy of the implementation.

**The cannon shell and the homing missile were about a tenth the size of what they were aimed at.** The reference is the world, not taste: the player's GRD LTV-45 is 36 units long and a lane is 17.5 wide, while the old missile measured roughly 8 units and the shell 7 — on an oblique camera that watches from a long way back. Both now measure **at least one lane long and at least a quarter-lane wide, and deliberately remain narrower than a lane**: a projectile wider than the lane it is aimed at would make dodging left or right meaningless, so "bigger" has a ceiling as well as a floor. Smoke measures the built meshes against `laneWidth` from config in both directions.

Size alone is not readability from directly above, so both also gained cues that read in silhouette: **hazard bands** (a grey tube over grey asphalt is nearly invisible from the top), fatter fins on the missile, a glowing drive belt on the shell, and a **pulsing exhaust flare** on each, so an incoming round flickers instead of gliding in silently. The pulse only rescales a cone that already exists on the pooled mesh, so nothing is allocated while the duel runs and the no-mid-game-shader-recompile invariant is untouched.

**The two projectiles now read as different weapons (2026-08-18, follow-up: *"bedakan bentuk homming missile dan canon"*).** After the size pass both were still the same silhouette — a tube with a cone on the front — differing only in length. The distinction is not decorative, because the two behave differently in a way the player has to act on: **the missile can be shot down** (it carries `missileHp` and is tested in `projectileHits`), while **the cannon shell can only be dodged**. Telling them apart at a glance is therefore a gameplay requirement, and the visual language follows it:

| | Homing missile | Cannon shell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silhouette | slender, aspect **4.28** | stubby, aspect **2.33** |
| Fins | cruciform tail fins + canards | **none at all** |
| Nose | sharp ogive + seeker head | blunt ogive + hot cap |
| Marking | **red hazard bands** — "you can destroy this" | **amber-glowing driving bands** — "unstoppable, leave the lane" |
| Motion | banks smoothly as it homes | **spins on its own axis** (`SHELL_SPIN`) |

Aspect ratio does the heavy lifting because it is the first thing legible from an oblique camera watching from a long way back — long before any surface detail. The two are held apart deliberately: missile ≥ 3.5, shell ≤ 3.0, and the missile at least 1.4× the shell. Smoke measures all three of the distinctions from the built meshes — the aspect band, finned-versus-finless, and `PAL.hazard` being exclusive to the missile so the red marking keeps its meaning.

### Every vehicle shatters, through one shared system (2026-08-18, user requests)

> *"buat agar saat player mati, mobil GRD LTV-45 meledak dan hancur berkeping-keping."* — then, the same day: *"buat agar mobil yang dikendarai musuh juga hancur berkeping-keping."*

The second request is what decided the shape of the first one's code. There are three vehicles in Stage 8 — the player's GRD LTV-45, the Raven-K carrier and the VULTURE-B barrel hauler — and three copies of a wreck routine is exactly the failure this repo has hit before (the occlusion fade once had three divergent versions). So the behaviour lives in **`entities/vehicleWreck.js`**, and each vehicle module only names what is specific to it: which children genuinely come off, and which are untouchable.

`shatterVehicle(rig, {group, materials, loose, skip, force, tilt, sink})` and `restoreVehicle(rig)` carry four rules, all inherited from `wreckSpawnMachine`'s contract:

1. **No mesh, material or PointLight is created.** Every shard is one of the rig's own children thrown out of place and every surface is charred in place, so a vehicle exploding can never trigger a shader recompile — and in the player-death case that is the single most sensitive moment in the game.
2. **`loose` parts fly; everything else only twists and sinks.** A wreck whose every piece has scattered stops reading as a vehicle. The LTV-45's doors, hatch leaves, sensor, weapon mount, bull bar and wheels come off; the carrier's wheels; the hauler's tailgate, remaining drums and wheels.
3. **Yaw is always preserved, and the scatter is a deterministic hash.** A carrier can enter facing either way, so zeroing its yaw would spin the wreck in place; and a deterministic shape is testable and never touches global RNG.
4. **Restoration is exact, not approximate.** Every one of these rigs is reused — carriers come from a fixed pool, haulers reuse two truck slots, and the stage replays whenever the player dies. That extends to the paths that clear a vehicle *while* it is still a wreck: `clearBarrelDroppers` (called when the gunship intro starts) restores too, or the next hauler is born charred and in pieces.

Per-vehicle specifics: the LTV-45 skips `gunnerMount`, which is the anchor the avatar is posed from — moving it would drag Major Gibran's dying body along. The carrier passes `sink: 0` and its current tilt, because `updateEnemyPickupVisual` already eases a wreck onto its side frame by frame, and it stops spinning its wheels once shattered — the wheels have come off their axles. The hauler does the same while its wreck brakes to a stop on the asphalt.

Each kill site adds its own explosion and gib burst, always with **black oil decals** rather than the default green, because only robots bleed coolant.

Smoke guards it from both directions every tick of the pursuit: every wrecked carrier must be shattered and charred, and every *live* carrier must be intact and in its original paint — which is what proves a recycled pool entry was genuinely restored rather than reborn as a burnt wreck. A source sweep rejects any per-vehicle copy of the routine.

### The player vehicle dies with the player (2026-08-18, user request)

> *"buat agar saat player mati, mobil GRD LTV-45 meledak dan hancur berkeping-keping."*

**One new shared hook: `activeScene.onPlayerDeath(dx, dz)`**, called exactly once from `startPlayerDeath` in `core/game.js`. What dies alongside the player is scene business, so it goes behind a hook rather than into the shared death path as a mode `if`. It receives the same normalised push direction the avatar's collapse uses, so the body plates fly the way the killing blow pushed.

**Why it is one call and not a per-frame animation.** `updateGame` gates the scene tick on `!dying` — `updateMode` is deliberately *not* called during the death sequence. So Stage 8 has no frame loop of its own while the player is dying, and the continuing motion has to ride the systems that *are* still ticked: `updateGore` (ballistic gibs) and `updateExplosions`. Stage 8 therefore fires two fireballs (one at the cabin, one low under the chassis — a single blast at one point reads flat from the oblique camera), a ground puff, camera shake, and 36 gibs in three bursts, with **black oil decals** rather than the default green, because only robots bleed coolant.

**The chassis is a one-shot pose, not a new object.** `wreckTacticalVehicle` in `entities/tacticalVehicle.js` follows `wreckSpawnMachine`'s contract exactly: **no mesh, material or PointLight is created**, so a vehicle exploding still cannot trigger a shader recompile at the single most sensitive moment in the game. Every shard is one of the rig's own children thrown out of place, split by whether it would physically come off — doors, hatch leaves, sensor, weapon mount, bull bar and wheels fly; body plates only twist and sink, because a wreck whose every piece has scattered stops reading as a vehicle. `gunnerMount` is deliberately skipped: it is the anchor the scene poses the avatar from, and moving it would drag Major Gibran's dying body with it. The whole group then settles tilted on its broken axles, and every material is charred in place.

**Restoration is exact, because dying means replaying the stage.** Every displaced transform and every original colour/emissive/opacity is saved at wreck time and restored by `resetTacticalVehicleVisual`, which the stage's `enter()` already calls. `updateTacticalVehicleVisual` early-returns on a wrecked rig, so nothing animates a corpse. Smoke pins both directions: the wreck really scatters (pose checksum moves, chassis tilts, paint changes, gibs land on the vehicle) with the child count unchanged, and the restore returns the pose checksum to its baseline bit-for-bit.

### Combat leftovers ride the road, not the vehicle (2026-08-17, user request)

> *"buat agar ketika robot dan mobilnya hancur, serpihan mereka tertinggal di tempat. tidak ikut bergerak seperti sekarang ini."*

Stage 8 is a coordinate-stable arena: the GRD LTV-45 stands still at `PLAYER_X` and the road, scenery and carriers scroll past it. Anything left at its world coordinates is therefore stationary **relative to the vehicle**, and on screen it reads as being dragged along at 92 units/second forever. That is what happened to every robot gib, corpse, bisected half and coolant pool, and to the debris thrown by an exploding carrier.

`updateRoad` now calls **`driftGore(dx)`** with exactly the same `dx` the road modules and the landscape pools use, so leftovers recede at precisely ground speed and are genuinely left behind on the asphalt. This is the identical problem — and the identical helper — as the Stage 5 journey (2026-08-09).

Two differences from Stage 5 are deliberate. **There is no `keep` exclusion**: the train had a car floor that legitimately travels with the player, but nothing rides the LTV, so even the armour plates thrown by `breakPlayerArmor()` fall to the road and are left behind. And **drops are untouched** — `clampDropPos` pins loot, ammo and medkits beside the vehicle on purpose, because in a coordinate-stable arena a drop that scrolled away could never be collected.

The carrier wreck itself also **brakes to a stop on the road**: its recession eases from 1.35× to 1.0× ground speed over half a second, so it settles onto the asphalt and recedes with it, instead of sliding backwards faster than the road surface forever. Smoke measures the wreck's recession every tick in multiples of ground speed and requires it to never drop below 1.0 (which would mean it is being carried along) and to actually settle at 1.0; a coolant decal, which has no velocity of its own, is used as the drift probe.

All tuning lives under `CFG.campaign.stage8`: road/lane timing, pickup cap/speed/wreck/resupply, entry inset/combat offset/spacing/approach rate, target/start delay/spawn gap/load cycle/boss approach delay, cutscene timing. There are no Stage 8 route-km/countdown keys. **The gunship's own stats live in `campaign.bosses.gunship` (2026-08-09, user request): it is a boss, so it sits beside `giant` and `tank` — including its own `hp` and `score`, which it used to borrow from `bosses.tank.hp`.** Only its scene pacing (`gunshipIntroMinSec`/`gunshipDeathDelaySec`/`arrivalMinSec`) stays under `stage8`, the same split as `campaign.tankOutro` vs `bosses.tank`. Public exports are `stage8Scene`, `ensureWorld()`, `worldBuilt()`, `S8_LANES`, `S8_START`, `S8_AIRPORT`, `STAGE8_DIALOGUE`, `stage8Walk()`, `stage8Debug()`, `stage8WorldDebug()`, `stage8ConvoyDebug()`, `stage8DialogueDebug()`, `stage8RoadDebug()`, pickup/gunship debug wrappers and `stage8StaticBatchDbg()`.

Pickup spawn X is always at least the matching endpoint of the twenty-module road pool, and is pushed farther out whenever `groundViewExtents()` plus config-driven `pickupOffscreenMargin` requires it for a wide display. This guarantees the pulled-back camera never sees a carrier being constructed.

The only thing left standing in the tank's path is the ruko that is built to come down.

Explicit props (`mkPropDecor`/`mkPropCover`) now record their footprints in `propRects`, and `roadsideDebug()` exposes the claimed decor rects, so the `S4 RUKO: koridor masuk tank BEBAS dekor` assert checks both **directly against `S4_LANE`** — the guarantee does not depend on `DECOR_ZONES` staying correct — and also verifies the lane really covers the whole path plus half a hull width.
The debris is deliberately fast but **low**: it settles at the foot of the building instead of scattering into the square where the duel happens (asserted). The building is pure decor — outside the walkable union, never in `blockers` or the nav grid, and excluded from static batching because its parts move — so destroying it cannot touch collision or pathing. `stage4.enter()` stands it back up on restart.
**How the camera is driven.** Exactly the same mechanism as the other two cutscenes: `setShotCam(az, dist, height, fogNear, fogFar)` writes a *target*, and `settleAll(dt, snap)` — one call at the end of `runCutscene`, so no shot boundary can escape it — damps the camera, the fog and the focus point toward it. The renderer reads it through the existing per-scene hook: stage 4 exposes `get camOffset() { return intro.camOffset(); }`, which returns `null` outside the cutscene so gameplay keeps `CAM_OFF_DEFAULT` untouched. Azimuths are written as `az(delta)`, a deviation from the gameplay angle (never copy the numbers), which is what makes shots 1 and 11 literally identical to the gameplay camera.

**Cuts.** `cutTo(shot, fx, fz)` moves angle and focus in the same frame with no motion between — there are exactly **four** (into `tremor`, `lock`, `fire`, and the impact). Everything else is damped motion; the smoke suite measures both (4 cuts, and every other frame under 12 units of camera and focus movement).

**The focus point** is damped here and then written with `setCineFocus(x, z, true)`. The renderer's own 1.5/s ease is too slow and too uniform for per-shot camera language, and snapping lets a cut actually be a cut. Two places deliberately do NOT track their subject: `reveal` holds the frame on where the tank is *going* (a subject charging the lens should enter a frame that is already waiting for it — tracking it dragged the focus 270 units north in a blink), and `advance` eases off the wreck onto the tank instead of snapping to it.

**Other pieces:** the tank's entry is one continuous line `TANK_FROM` → `TANK_FIRE` crossed across both `tremor` and `reveal`, so there is no speed break at the shot boundary (measured: 0.05 vs a 3.6 peak); `turretForward()` keeps the gun aligned with the hull on the way in (`turretYaw = hullYaw − π/2`, per the tank.js contract); the per-shot fog is saved in `start()` and restored in `endCutscene`/`reset`; the four extraction lines use the shared stage-radio typewriter instead of generic captions; and `faceAction` overrides `aimPoint` so the player avatar turns to watch the action.

Guarded by the `S4 CUTSCENE *` asserts in the smoke suite, which were checked against the old behaviour: removing the camera hook and the hit-stop fails four of them, and flattening the telegraph / the heli lift / the turret lock / the per-shot fog fails four more.
## Tank-boss death sequence — the turret blow-off (rebuilt 2026-07-29)

The old death was one `explodeAt`, an instant colour swap to charred paint, and a turret that merely floated up 3.5 units and tilted 0.32 rad in place — the kill of a 1800-HP boss read as smaller than a barrel popping. It is now a **three-beat director** in `entities/tank.js` (`tank.deathPhase`, driven by `updateDeath`); the beats and their order are the contract, the amplitudes are visual constants in code (not config).

**Beat 1 — `'cook'` cook-off (`DEATH_COOK_SEC` 0.65 s).** The tank does *not* blow up on the killing shot: it takes a short hit-stop punch, one internal detonation on the engine deck, and then shudders in place with a growing amplitude while the paint darkens **progressively** toward `CHAR_HEX` (`applyChar`, the same "recolour the existing Lambert materials" trick as the hit flash — no new material). Fire spits from the seams, black smoke rises, small ammo cook-offs pop inside the hull, and the camera shake ramps 1 → 3.4. This anticipation beat is what the old version lacked entirely.

**Beat 2 — `'fly'` the turret comes off (`blowTurret`, the money shot; the user's idea).** The fireball fires at the turret ring, then the turret is **reparented from the hull group to the scene** (world transform preserved, hull scale reapplied) and becomes a free rigid body in `tank.turretFly`: launched **perpendicular to the hull's muzzle axis**, on the side **facing away from the player** so it never sails across the camera, with a small random component along the hull so it isn't always dead-square. It tumbles (`rotation.x/z` spin), trails smoke and sparks, **bounces once** on landing (`TURRET_BOUNCES`) and then **keels over onto its side** (`restZ` ±1.35 rad, lerped over `restK`). `addHitStop(0.5, 0.25)` holds the moment in slow motion — hit-stop is the project's one sanctioned slowdown source, so this is a factor inside `globalTimeScale()`, not a second time scale.

**Beat 3 — `'wreck'` the hull settles (`updateWreck`).** The hull slams down and rocks with a damped oscillation until it rests sunk ~1 unit and canted at `WRECK_TILT` — at its **true death position**, not at whatever jitter offset the cook-off shudder ended on. Fire keeps venting from the open turret ring, thinning out over `WRECK_BURN_SEC` 3.2 s into a plain smoke column.

The detached turret is no longer a child of `parts.group`, so **`disposeTank` removes it separately** (otherwise it stays floating in the scene for the rest of the run). The former `stage4.WIN_DELAY_SEC` countdown is now removed: the new boss-outro close-up owns this same death sequence and waits for its `wreck` phase before cutting to Gibran, so MISSION COMPLETE cannot interrupt the turret flight. Every FX call rides an existing pool (`explodeAt`, gibs, ground puffs, blood sprites), so the "no mid-game shader recompile" rule still holds. Guarded by the `S4 MATI SINEMATIK *` and `S4 OUTRO *` asserts (beat order, turret detached and landed beside the hull on the far side from the player, hull settled and tilted, paint fully charred, exact dialogue/typewriter order, fade, and detached-turret disposal).

## The hacking minigame — "ICE BREACH" (2026-07-28)

Hacking used to be *stand still and watch a progress bar* — 10 s at the Stage 1 mainframe, `hackSec` at each Stage 3 terminal. The user replaced the wait with a puzzle: **`src/scenes/campaign/utility/hackMinigame.js`**, a modal scene (`hackScene`, id `campaign-hack`) that takes over the screen.

**The puzzle.** A fixed 5×5 board of circuit chips in `#hackOverlay` (inline SVG + CSS, no assets). Left-click a chip to rotate it 90° clockwise, right-click to rotate it back. Power flows from the **INGRESS port** on the middle-left cell; every chip it reaches lights up amber (the rest stay dim teal), and the board is solved when the current reaches the **DATA CORE** on the middle-right cell. Visible external traces bridge INGRESS directly to the left tile and the right tile directly to DATA CORE, aligned with the middle row; the output trace changes from teal to amber when powered. An **ICE TRACE** countdown (`traceSec`) runs along the bottom: hit zero and the terminal locks you out.

**Always solvable, never pre-solved.** `buildPuzzle` draws a random port→core path first (randomised DFS), gives every path cell exactly the shape it needs, fills the remaining cells with decoy chips (elbow/straight/T/stub at `decoyChance`, the rest dead chips that cannot be clicked), then scrambles every rotation — with each *path* chip guaranteed to start in a wrong orientation, so the player has to touch all of them and a board never opens already finished.

**The modal contract** mirrors the campaign shop scene: `enter()` pauses the game and releases pointer lock (so the OS cursor can click), `shopActive()` returns true so input.js swallows gameplay keys and never raises the pause menu, and every gameplay hook is a no-op — except `groundHeight`, which returns the `feetY` it was given rather than 0, because the modal is installed **mid-frame** from a stage's `updateMode` and the rest of that frame must not drop the player through the floor. Escape (or the ABORT button) ends it as `'abort'`; the trace running out ends it as `'fail'`; solving it shows **ACCESS GRANTED** and then calls `onSuccess`.

**Coming back** is the part that needed a new primitive. `setScene(stage)` would call the stage's `enter()` and reset the whole floor, so `finish()` uses **`resumeScene(prevScene)`** (core/sceneManager.js) instead — activeScene is restored without any lifecycle call, then the callback runs, then `requestLock()` puts the player straight back in the fight (with a safety net: if the browser refuses the lock, the normal "click to continue" blocker appears instead of a silent pause).

**Failing the trace sets off the ALARM** (2026-07-28, user request). The banner reads **ALARM TRIGGERED — LOCKED OUT**, the modal closes, and the stage answers with two things: a **hunter squad** — `alarmHordeCount` class C robots that spawn and immediately chase you — and a **`alarmCooldownSec` lockout** on that terminal, shown counting down in the HUD status line, so you get the room clear before you can try again. Aborting deliberately (ESC) does neither: it just puts you back where you were.

The horde spawns **off-camera** on purpose — nothing pops into existence on screen. `spawnAlarmHorde` / `offscreenSpawnPoints` (campaign/utility/common.js) sample a ring around the player, one randomised direction at a time so they arrive from several sides, and only accept a point that is both walkable for that stage and outside the camera's ground footprint (`camFocusPos()` + `groundViewExtents()`, plus a margin). Indoor stages simply reject most candidates against the walkable grid and the robots then path in through the corridors. If at least one valid off-screen point exists but there are too few unique points, the remainder reuse those valid anchors and robot separation spreads them afterward; a visible fallback must never leak one robot into view. Stage-owned fallback cells are used only when the sampler finds no valid off-screen point at all.

**Stage 1** uses the same `gridSize` board as every Stage 3 terminal; aborting drops back to the `download` phase and, like Stage 3, the trigger re-arms only after you step away from the terminal (`s1CompArmed`). Tuning lives in `CFG.campaign.hack` = `gridSize`, `traceSec`, `decoyChance`, `alarmHordeCount`, `alarmCooldownSec`, `alarmSpawnMinUnits`, `alarmSpawnMaxUnits`. `CFG.campaign.stage1.downloadSec` is dormant.

Guarded by the `HACK` asserts in the smoke suite (all callers resolve to `gridSize`, generator always solvable, both external traces exist and follow power state, every path chip starts wrong, rotation round-trip, dead chips inert, modal swallows input and cannot open twice, ESC abort, ICE TRACE timeout) plus the `S1 HACK` / `S3 HACK` integration asserts and the `S1 ALARM` / `S3 ALARM` ones (exact horde size, all class C and chasing, **every spawn outside the camera footprint**, terminal locked for the cooldown, and hackable again once the horde is dead, the player has stepped away and the cooldown has run out).

## The generator repair minigame — "FIELD REPAIR" (2026-07-29)

Firing up the Stage 2 generator used to be *stand on the marker and watch a 10 s bar* (`restoreSec`, movement frozen). **The three boards** (`REPAIR_PARTS`, played in this order; the same list names each component when you pick it up in the warehouse, so the thing you carried and the board you play are the same object):

1. **POWER HARNESS — splice the wires.** A column of FEED pins on the left, a column of BUS terminals on the right in a shuffled order. **Drag one end onto the other, or click one end then the other**; click a finished cable to pull it. **A mismatched splice is rejected** (on the click route the picked end stays selected so you can try the next terminal), so the board can never be driven into a dead end — the colour pairing is a bijection and every wire has exactly one home.
2. **CONTROL BOARD — fit the chips.** Chips of different sizes in a tray, sockets of the same sizes on the board, both shuffled. **Drag a chip into its socket, or click the chip then the socket**; a chip only drops into a socket of exactly its size, and clicking a seated chip — or dragging it back onto the tray — lifts it out. Sizes are drawn from a pool where every (w, h) pair is distinct, so one chip = one socket. The socket board is drawn on a unit grid so sizes can be counted, not eyeballed.
3. **COOLANT PUMP — set the valves.** A row of dials, each with an amber needle and a green target notch. Left-click a dial (or `+`) turns it up, right-click (or `−`) turns it down — but the valves are **geared: turning one also turns every valve to its RIGHT**. That gearing is triangular, which is what guarantees the board: turning valve *i* never disturbs valve *i−1*, so working left to right always lands on the one and only solution.

**Board size follows difficulty** — `CFG.campaign.repair.count` = **3 (easy) / 4 (normal) / 5 (hard)** wires, chips or valves, read from the live `difficulty` binding in core/state.js. `CFG.campaign.repair.valveSteps` is the number of notches on each dial. `CFG.campaign.stage2.restoreSec` is now dormant (kept in the JSON).

**PHASE SYNC** (2026-08-19, user request — replaced FAULT ISOLATION, which had replaced START INTERLOCK, which had replaced FUSE LOADOUT) is a SYNCHRONISATION board: an oscilloscope shows a bright bus reference trace with one trace per phase running against it, and a slider under each phase. Slide each phase's trim until its wave lies on the reference and the scope collapses into a single steady line; every lamp goes green, the panel reads ALL PHASES SYNCHRONISED and the turbine spools up. Board size is `syncPhases` (3/3/4 by difficulty).

**The only difficulty is COUPLING.** Moving one slider drags the others a fraction of the same distance (`syncCoupling`), exactly as real machines load each other — so a single pass down the panel never finishes it, and the player comes back to trim again. Measured against the natural strategy (set each slider to its mark, repeat), every board converges in **at most two sweeps**.

Why three boards died before this one. Each failed differently and the replacement is shaped by all three:

- **FUSE LOADOUT** — its answer was the constant `FUSE_AMPS.slice(2, 2+n)`. This board is played four times per run (Stage 5 once, Stage 6 three times), so three of those were pure recall. Rule: **a repeated board must generate its answer.**
- **START INTERLOCK** — generated its answer correctly, but every requirement was printed on the breaker card and a wrong close was free. With all the information on screen and mis-clicks costless, clicking every row repeatedly was the *fastest* strategy. Rule: **a board whose optimal play is brute force has no tension.**
- **FAULT ISOLATION** — a sound deduction puzzle, and rejected for being *"terlalu rumit"*. Tree, meter, probes, PAR: all of it had to be read and held in the head. Rule, and the one that shapes this board: **this is a generator repair in the middle of a combat stage, not a logic puzzle — it has to be understood by looking at it.**

So Phase Sync has **no hidden information at all and nothing to read**. What is out of alignment looks out of alignment. Four rules hold it:

- **Everything on the panel derives from one quantity** — `syncError(g, i)`, the slider's distance from its mark. Frequency readout, amplitude readout, lamp colour, and the trace's own frequency/amplitude/phase all come from it, so the display cannot disagree with the model. Land on the mark and the readout is exactly the bus's 50.0 Hz at 100%.
- **Always solvable, and proven rather than hoped.** Setting slider *i* exactly and moving on is Gauss–Seidel on a matrix with unit diagonal and off-diagonal `c`; it converges when the matrix is strictly diagonally dominant, i.e. `(n−1)·|c| < 1`. `syncCoupling(n)` **clamps the configured value to 0.9 of that bound**, so no retune the user types can make a board unsolvable — and smoke runs the natural strategy across 200 boards to prove it. Targets are also kept away from both ends of the track so coupling can never push the answer out of a slider's range.
- **No countdown and no way to lose** (the 2026-07-29 rule): a bad slide is simply slid back.
- **The scrolling is purely visual.** The waves roll under their own `setInterval` — the modal owns it because the game loop is paused, the same reason ICE BREACH owns its countdown — and the tick touches no gameplay value, so a board can never solve itself while being watched. Repaints are light: the scope's own markup plus the kept slider references, never a full `renderBoard`, which would rip the slider out from under the cursor mid-drag (the same lesson as `paintRotor`).

Both the slider track and a drag are live: clicking anywhere on a track jumps the trim there, and dragging follows the cursor — the same click-and-drag pairing the wire and chip boards have had since 2026-07-29. The win plays the existing turbine asset over the normal success tone; no new audio was added for one board.

**ROTOR KICKSTART** uses a large draggable flywheel: clockwise motion builds RPM, ignition only catches inside the green band, and the master breaker becomes available after combustion stabilises. Bad ignition timing retains a config-driven fraction of RPM instead of resetting the board. A CRANK button provides a fallback for the circular drag. **The flywheel bleeds `rotorDecayPerSec` RPM per second while cranking** (2026-08-19) — before that, RPM never fell, so the green band was not a window at all: the board was `crank N times → ignition → breaker`, with no timing whatsoever behind the panel's own "fire ignition inside the green RPM band". Decay is applied from the modal's own `setInterval` and repaints only the needle/dial/state through `paintRotor`, never the whole board. Two tuning invariants are smoke-asserted from `CFG` rather than pinned to numbers: one CRANK click must raise RPM by LESS than the band's width (otherwise the button-only path can step over the band and never land inside it), and `bandWidth / rotorDecayPerSec` must remain a human reaction window (currently 2.0 s).

The pair reads as one job done with the hands: **line the phases up, then spin the machine up.** Both are driven by dragging, both are judged by a picture rather than by text, and neither has a countdown or a fail state.

Both boards are smoke-proven solvable and have no way to lose — only ABORT. Progress is committed after each board through `onProgress`, so aborting after PHASE SYNC resumes at ROTOR KICKSTART. Tuning is under `CFG.campaign.repair.advanced` using the `sync*` and `rotor*` keys.

The user asked for a sweep: *"pastikan jika ada object yang menghalangi player ATAU robot, maka object itu akan jadi transparan … saya ingin transparannya menjadi 20%"* — and reported that the behaviour only looked consistent in Stages 1–4.

That was accurate. There were **three unrelated implementations**:

| Where | Test | Fade | Robots counted? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stage 4 | camera→entity line of sight | 0.45 | yes |
| Stage 11 | distance from player to tree | 0.18 | no |
| Stage 13 | distance from player to tree | 0.42 | no |
| Stages 5–10, 12 | *(none)* | — | — |

All three are gone. `src/scenes/campaign/utility/occlusion.js` is now the only occluder registry, and `CFG.campaign.occlusion.opacity` (**0.2**) is the only fade value. The API and its cost model are catalogued in [MODULES.md](MODULES.md#addendum-shared-occlusion-fade--stages-113-2026-08-13); what follows is why the design is the way it is.

### Line of sight, and a half-body threshold

The camera looks down from the south-west at a fixed pitch, so the only thing that can hide an entity is a prop **on the camera side of it** that is tall enough to cut the sight line *at that distance*. A radius test around the player is wrong in both directions: it fades props standing behind the player (which block nothing) and misses a tall prop 60 units away that is squarely in the way.

The threshold is the user's second request (2026-08-14): *"object apapun yang menghalangi akan menjadi transparan jika minimal menutupi setengah badan character."* So the test measures **how much of the body is hidden**, not merely whether something is in the way:

```
tEnter = entry of the entity→camera ray into the prop footprint   (2-D slab test,
                                                                   box widened by lateralPad)
cover  = (prop.top − entityFeetY) − slope · tEnter
fades  = cover ≥ bodyHeight × coverFraction          (coverFraction 0.5)
```

`slope = CAM_OFF.y / |CAM_OFF.xz|` comes from the `camOffsetActive()` export, so Stage 3's north-west view and Stages 7/8's pulled-back offsets are handled without a per-stage copy of the constant. `bodyHeight` derives from `CFG.player.eyeHeight`; a robot's threshold scales with its own mesh scale, so a class-A frame needs more cover than a class-C one.

Two details in that formula are load-bearing. **The ray is intersected with the footprint, not compared against the distance to the prop's centre** — a 4.8 m car occludes from its *rear face*, several metres nearer than its centre, and (the opposite failure) Stage 10's 180-unit warehouse shell must not count as covering somebody standing 100 units off to the side just because they are inside its bounding radius; the ray simply never enters that footprint. And **props that only cover the legs now stay opaque**, which is the visible difference from the first pass: a low sedan no longer flickers as you brush past it.

### Materials are cloned, and props are welded into themselves

Two rules make this affordable and safe:

1. **`registerOccluder` clones the object's materials.** Most stage decor shares a handful of `M.*` materials across the whole world; fading the shared instance would fade the entire batch. The clones are created during world build, while the loading screen is still up, so the no-mid-game-recompile invariant is untouched (`transparent`/`opacity` are not part of the r128 program key).
2. **`weldOccluder` = `mergeObjectInPlace` + register.** A prop that must fade cannot live inside `addMergedStatic`'s big per-material batch, but it also must not go back to being 20–40 loose meshes. Welding it into *itself* keeps its own transform and material instances at a cost of a few draw calls, and — because each prop is now its own frustum-cullable node — culling gets *finer*, not coarser.

That second rule is what let Stage 7 change from a per-125 m vehicle batch to **one welded node per vehicle** for all 240 cars. The chunk binning is still computed and still reported through `stage7WorldDebug().optimization.vehicleChunks` (nothing may ever become one route-wide batch), but a single bus can now fade on its own.

### Moving occluders

Props that travel — the Iron Port crane's carried containers — register with `dynamic: true`. They stay out of the uniform grid, their `x`/`z` are re-read every frame, and their effective `top` rises with `position.y` so a container held high still reads as an obstruction.

### Stage 8 is deliberately empty

Stage 8 registers no occluders. Only the camera side (+z) can ever occlude, and its landscape band starts at z 86 while the player is locked to the carriageways at |z| ≤ 52.5. Even in the worst case — the player in the outermost right lane — the eye→player sight line is already at y ≈ 52 where it crosses z 86 and climbs steeply beyond it, while the tallest thing in that band (a shophouse with its hip roof, or a coconut palm) tops out around 44 and the taller items sit deeper still. Everything else lives on the backdrop side, which is behind the player from the camera's point of view. Adding occluders would be dead weight. If Stage 8 ever gains scenery inside the carriageway band, or raises the +z band's height, it needs the same treatment as everyone else.

### What each stage registers

Stage 1/2 the barricade piles (`buildFurniturePile` gained an `onGroup` callback so the shared token builder stays the single implementation); Stage 3 the two factory machines (they are sunk below the opaque floor before deploy, so fading them there is a no-op); Stage 4 its cars and roadside buildings, unchanged apart from the fade value; Stage 5 every depot/platform furniture piece plus the generator, the C1 terminal and the spawn machine; Stage 6 Arrival its shelves, inspection benches, distribution panels, key racks, generators and fabricators; Stage 6 HQ every `C` server bank and both fabricators; Stage 7 all 240 vehicles; Stage 9 the control tower, hangar, operations building, twelve ground-support vehicles, six light masts and twelve cargo pallets; Stage 10 the container stacks, forklifts, reach stacker, warehouse shell, pipe-rack bents, process tanks and the crane-carried containers; Stage 11 the hero trees, both shelters, the carrier wreck, the sensor masts and the intake house; Stage 12 the civic cover and colonnade on the surface and the twelve capacitor banks in the root chamber; Stage 13 the foreground rain trees, the 28 stalled vehicles on the deployment avenue and **Monas itself** — it stands in the middle of the boss arena and is the single largest thing that can swallow the player.


### Walls fade too (2026-08-14, user request)

*"saya ingin tembok/dinding juga transparant jika menutupi character."* Walls are the most common occluder in an indoor stage and by far the hardest to fade one cell at a time:

- Stages 1–3 put every `#` cell in a single `InstancedMesh`. One material, one draw call — fading it turns the whole building to glass.
- Stages 5 and 6 melted the cell body **and** its `buildDetailedWallCell` skin into the shared `addMergedStatic` batch, which has no way to give one cell back.

`campaign/utility/wallFade.js` solves both with the same trick: **a wall cell is never faded — it is swapped.** The cell is hidden from its instanced mesh (its matrix is scaled to zero) and a stand-alone **proxy** from a small pool takes its place, carrying its own cloned materials so each cell eases on its own curve. The pool is 12 slots, far more than the number of cells that can cover the player plus nearby robots at once, and idle slots are invisible and cost nothing.

The face skin is handled the same way. `buildDetailedWallCell` emits identical geometry for every exposed face — only the position differs — so it is recorded once on a canonical cell and turned into **instanced meshes per (face direction × material)**. That is what lets the skin vanish with its cell; without it the translucent proxy would stand behind still-opaque panels. The fixed cost per stage is one body mesh plus at most 16 skin meshes, guarded by smoke at ≤ 17 draw groups — not one welded group per cell, which would have been thousands.

Two rules came out of it:

1. **Re-identify the matrix before showing a cell again.** `Matrix4.setPosition` only writes the translation column, so reusing the matrix that just zero-scaled a cell restores it at scale 0 and the wall disappears for good. `placeMatrix()` calls `identity()` first, and the smoke `Matrix4`/`InstancedMesh` stubs now record scale and position so the round-trip is a real (mutation-tested) assert rather than an assumption.
2. **Proxy materials are built with the world, never on demand.** `renderer.compile` walks the entire scene including hidden objects, so the proxies are warm before the loading screen clears and the no-mid-game-shader-recompile invariant is untouched.

Adopted by Stage 1 (452 cells), Stage 2 (386), Stage 3 (293), Stage 5's station (175), Stage 6 Arrival (385) and Stage 6 HQ (412). Stage 5's `@` windowed wall cells become welded occluders instead, with their glass pane left as the separate transparent mesh it already was. Stage 12's root chamber registers its twenty 94-unit buttresses.

With walls included, the old note that "interior walls are deliberately not occluders" is retired — it was true only while there was no affordable way to fade one cell.



## Robot density multiplier + Stage 1 door override (2026-08-16, user requests)

Two independent changes that landed together.

### `robotCountMul` — one number per stage

`CFG.campaign.stageN.robotCountMul` scales the **entire population a stage places itself**: the opening garrison, every reinforcement wave, and the horde. The requested values are Stage 1 **1.5**, Stage 2 **1.6**, Stage 3 **1.3**, Stage 4 **2**, Stage 5's **station chapter** 1.5 and Stage 6 **2** (both chapters). A stage without the key is unaffected — Stages 7–13 still run at 1×, so this is opt-in rather than a global difficulty knob.

Not one spawn-point table was edited. Three helpers in `campaign/utility/common.js` are the only readers:

- **`stageRobotMul(stage)`** — the config lookup, defaulting to 1.
- **`scaleRobotCount(n, stage)`** — a single number (Stage 3's `gateWaveCount`/`machineWaveCount`, Stage 1's `hordeCount`). A table that originally held robots can never round down to zero.
- **`scaleSpawnCounts(counts, stage)`** — a row of counts: per spawn point, or per class in a `{C,B,A}` encounter. It rounds **cumulatively**, so the total is exactly `round(total × mul)` while each entry stays proportional. Rounding each entry on its own was tried and rejected: `3 × 1.5 = 4.5` rounds *up* at every single spot, which inflates the total well past the requested percentage and quietly rewrites an encounter's class mix.

Where each stage hooks in:

| Stage | Scaled | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | wave-1 garrison, wave-2, horde | per-spot counts; `S1_WAVE2` entries keep their own class when copied |
| 2 | wave-1, warehouse guards, wave-2 | same pattern; `s2Wave1Base` 50 → 80 |
| 3 | gate waves + machine waves | the stage's *whole* population is waves |
| 4 | the 13 spot table | class pattern repeats with `k % n` |
| 5 | station depot garrison | `s5DepotCounts()` scales C/B/A together |
| 6 | all six encounter tables, both chapters | one call site, `spawnEncounter` in `runtime.js` |

Two things are deliberately outside the multiplier. **Spawn machines and fabricators** (Stages 3, 5, 6, 7) print until they are destroyed — that is a rate, already fenced by `machineMaxAlive`, not a count; multiplying it would change the pressure curve rather than the number of enemies. And Stage 5's **enemy consist** is `ET_CARGO_CARS`, a geometry constant whose meshes are preallocated.

Stage 4 needed one extra rule: its per-spot class list (`S4_RANGED`) used to be indexed straight by `k`, with everything past the end falling back to class C. Doubling a spot that way would have halved the share of A/B shooters that were deliberately raised on 2026-07-19, so the index now wraps (`k % n`) and repeats the original pattern instead.

### Stage 1: the kill-switch file opens every locked door

A successful mainframe hack — the moment the kill-switch file is in hand — now hands the player the building's door control. `overrideDoorLocks(doors)` in `campaign/utility/doors.js` clears `locked` **and** `broken` on every door that is still shut, greens its jamb lamps, and returns how many it freed (surfaced as `doorsFreed`/`lockedDoors` in `s1Debug()`). In practice that is the two `+` doors, since the NAC door was already opened by the `@` security bank earlier — and those two are exactly the point: they are the permanently jammed shortcuts, and they open on the same frame the horde spawns, so the fight back to the stairs has routes it did not have on the way in.

Three details make it work:

1. **Clearing `broken` is enough to restore motion.** A broken door's `open` is pinned at `DOOR_BROKEN_AJAR` at build time and its leaves are already drawn in that pose, so `updateStageDoors` — which used to `continue` past it — simply resumes from there and eases to fully open. Nothing is rebuilt, no material is created.
2. **The lamp really can turn green.** A door that starts locked gets its own `lockMat` instance (an unlocked door shares the global green material), so recoloring it cannot bleed into every other door.
3. **The override must not survive the run.** `buildStageDoors` records `baseLocked`/`baseBroken`, and `stage1Scene.enter()` calls **`resetDoorLocks(doors)`** — every door back to its map state, closed, red where the map says red. Death and Pause → Restart both replay Stage 1, so without this the second run would start with the shortcuts already open.

Robots and pathfinding need no special handling: `doorsWalkable` reads the live `open` value, so a freed door becomes a route for A* the moment it slides, and nav is never rebaked.
