- **Only the active stage's lamps are lit** (2026-07-26, after stage 4 still felt heavy with the meshes already batched). The four campaign worlds share one `THREE.Scene`, and Three.js forward-renders: every Lambert/Phong fragment loops over *every* point light in the scene, so all 54 room/street lamps were shaded on every pixel — 57 point lights, 45 of them belonging to stages tens of thousands of units away with intensity 0. Stage 4 suffered most because its screen is mostly ground (a 6000×3000 base plane plus stacked verges, sidewalks and markings). Each stage now registers its lamps under a key (`registerStageLight`), every scene declares a `lightsKey`, and `setScene` lights only that set: **stage 1 = 21 point lights, stages 2/3/4 = 15, survival = 3**. The explosion-light pool and muzzle flash are deliberately NOT registered so they work everywhere. Because the count now differs per stage, `precompileStageLightSets(scene)` runs once at the end of `stage1.ensureWorld` (still behind the loading screen) and compiles the shader programs for all four configurations up front — so the transition itself has no recompile hitch. Stage 4's big ground materials also dropped from Phong to Lambert: their `specular` was already near-black (0x0c0b09-0x141310), so the per-light specular term was invisible but still computed; the road and ring keep Phong because they need `normalMap`.

  **Stage 4 needs the second entry point, `mergeObjectInPlace(obj)`**: its cars and camera-side buildings are registered with `registerOccluder`, which reads `obj.position` and drives their material opacity to fade them out when they block the view — so they must keep their own transform and their own material instances. That call welds *within* one object (SUV 20 meshes → 8, sedan 17 → 7, each roadside lot 2-8 → 1-3) and leaves the fade behaviour byte-identical. Everything else that moves, toggles `visible` or changes colour (doors, shrouds, EXIT signs, markers, spawn machines, blast door, the road gate, crates, barrels, street lamps) stays a standalone object. **`classifyForBatch` guarantees nothing is thrown away**: the crate's amber `LineSegments` edge is merged into a single LineSegments, and PointLights (street lamps), sprites, InstancedMeshes (trees/skyline) and transparent glass are re-attached at their world position instead of being welded — an early version of the batcher silently dropped non-mesh children and lost the crate outlines. Rooms look exactly the same: a vertex-by-vertex diff against the unbatched path (run against real Three.js r128, outside the stubbed harness) gives identical vertex counts on all four stages and a max deviation of 0,004 units ≈ 0,6 mm at game scale, purely float32 rounding of world coordinates out at x≈90.000-120.000.
- **Stage 1 is class C only** — the post-download wave-2 no longer mixes in B/A shooters.
- **Stage 2 has no class A** — the warehouse guards and the post-generator reinforcement wave use B instead.
- **Stage 3 spawns are STAGGERED and ANIMATED** — see the Stage 3 section.
- **Mission text carries no compass directions** ("south", "far-right room", …) — the player is meant to find objectives themselves. A smoke assert sweeps every `showStageMsg`/`showPickup`/`hudStatus` string in the four campaign stages for direction words.

## Stage 3 — Lantai Pabrik Robot

**Staggered spawn + materialize animation (2026-07-26, user request):** a wave is no longer dumped in one frame. `spawnDoorWave`/`spawnMachineWave` push entries onto `s3Queue`, and `s3TickSpawns(dt)` (called at the top of `updateMode`) releases ONE robot every `CFG.campaign.stage3.spawnGapSec` (0.3 s) from the stairwell / lift / machine hatch. Each released robot materializes: its MESH scales up from ~12% to full over `spawnRiseSec` (0.55 s) with a teal flash + dust puff at the exit point. This is visual only — `z.scl` (the hit-test scale) is never touched, so a robot mid-materialize is still fair to shoot; if it dies during the rise its mesh is snapped back to full scale so the corpse/gibs are not shrunken. `s3SpawnDbg()` exposes `{queued, rising}` for tests.

Two consequences for the wave logic: a new wave is only scheduled while `s3Queue` is EMPTY (waves never stack), and destroying a target cancels its pending spawns — blowing the blast door clears the whole queue ("gelombang STOP"), and destroying a machine removes just that machine's queued robots.
